Discipline became a real spec rather than a buff bot — but most of what is written about Priest is BlizzCon demo data that nobody has re-verified.
## Starting out
Priest is the game's archetypal healer. It wears cloth, has no pet and almost no melee presence; what it has is the broadest set of tools for keeping other people upright. A direct heal for repairing damage that has happened, a heal that ticks away over time, and a shield that stops damage before it lands rather than mending it afterwards. It also has a shadow-magic side that kills things perfectly well on its own, so you are not obliged to heal to reach level 60.
Healing suits people who like watching other people. In a group you spend the fight looking at four health bars and barely at the monster. Some players find that the most interesting job in the game; others find it thankless. You will know within two dungeons which you are.
At low level Priest is slow and fragile. You have few buttons, mana drains faster than you expect, individual kills take a while, and you sit down to drink a great deal. The compensation arrives the moment you group: healers are in short supply and you will rarely wait for an invitation. Be warned that the commonest way a Priest dies is watching everyone else's health and forgetting its own.
### What the specs do
| Spec | Role | In one line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discipline | Healer | Prevention and efficiency — shields, damage that never lands, and mana that lasts. |
| Holy | Healer | The pure healing tree, with the largest heals and the group-healing tools. |
| Shadow | Ranged DPS | Damage over time and mind magic, for killing rather than mending. |
You are not locked into anything. Talent points arrive one at a time as you level, so early on you have barely enough to matter, and your trainer will wipe them for a fee if you change your mind. Healing spells are trained regardless of which tree you feed, so a Shadow Priest can still heal a low dungeon. On roles, if you have never grouped: the tank holds the monster's attention so it hits the person built to survive it, the healer keeps everyone alive, and the DPS kill it. Groups will assume you are the healer.
### Your resource
Mana is the blue bar, and it is the whole game for a Priest. Every spell costs some. Nothing you do in combat puts it back except potions, so think of a fight as a budget rather than a rotation.
Out of combat it refills two ways. It trickles back on its own at a rate driven by your Spirit, and it refills quickly while you eat or drink. The trickle has a catch worth learning properly, usually called the five-second rule: finishing a cast switches that passive regeneration off for the next five seconds. Cast one spell after another and you receive almost none of it. Leave gaps and it resumes.
In practice this means three habits. Pause deliberately rather than filling every second with a cast. Use your wand on weak targets, because attacking with it costs nothing and lets regeneration keep running. And drink to a full bar between fights, every single time, even when standing still for thirty seconds feels wasteful. New Priests die with health to spare and no mana, having skipped that half-minute eight fights in a row.
### Your first twenty levels
Smite is your damage spell to begin with, and Shadow Word: Pain is your first damage-over-time effect — apply it, then Smite while it ticks. Lesser Heal, and later Heal, are your repair tools. Renew heals a little each second and is what you cast while moving or on chip damage. Power Word: Shield absorbs a set amount of damage and is best cast before you are hit, not after.
Power Word: Fortitude adds stamina and lasts a long while; cast it on yourself and on everyone in the group. Inner Fire improves your armour and is the buff newcomers forget after every death. Mind Blast is a harder-hitting shadow nuke on a short cooldown. Psychic Scream frightens nearby enemies into running away, which is how you survive a bad pull. Fade drops your threat and is the button that stops a monster chasing you across the room. You also learn to remove magic and disease, and to resurrect the dead.
The two most-ignored buttons in this stretch are Fade and your wand.
### Common beginner mistakes
- Drinking to roughly half a mana bar and pulling again. Drink to full.
- Casting your largest heal on a scratch. A small heal, or Renew, costs a fraction as much.
- Shielding after the tank is already hurt, instead of before the pull.
- Standing in melee range of the thing you are fighting because your spells reach that far.
- Watching the tank's health so closely that you never notice your own, and dying with three heals you could have cast on yourself.
- Forgetting Fortitude and Inner Fire after a death or a zone change.

## Read this before using anything below
**Two disjoint bodies of Priest information exist, and the detailed one is not the live one.**
The long talent-by-talent list below comes from the **BlizzCon demo build**. Its own source says so — the Priest changes are "based largely on the BlizzCon demo build" — and its changelog stops on 15 September, two days before the beta opened. Blizzard's live beta notes contain exactly **seven** Priest lines across every build to date, all small bug fixes.
So the rich material is pre-beta data nobody has re-verified, and the live data is thin. Items marked *(demo)* mean: Blizzard showed this in September, and nobody has confirmed the numbers survived into the shipping build.
The upside of that thinness is itself a finding: **Blizzard has not balance-touched Priest once during this beta.** No damage, mana or cooldown tuning to any Priest spell has been published.
A structural tell worth noticing: in Blizzard's own notes every other class is subdivided by **spec**. **Priest alone is subdivided by *race*.**
## The seven live changes
1. **Renew can now land critical hits** — framed as a bug fix, which means periodic healing crits *by design*. A real departure from 1.12.
2. **Power Word: Shield can always overwrite an existing shield** on a target without Weakened Soul.
3. **Starshards** correctly gains spell-pushback resistance from Twilight Focus.
4. **Dark Sacrifice** no longer breaks crowd control that breaks on damage, and its mana gain scales with Spirit.
5. Gnome's **Contingency Plan** can now crit.
6. Gnome's **Confounding Flash** got a cast visual, retuned Holy rather than Arcane.
7. **Eureka! was nerfed** to a flat 10% discount across every class — Priests had 15%.
## Talents made baseline
Blizzard named three "tax" talents going baseline game-wide — Divine Spirit, Blessing of Kings and Improved Mark of the Wild — and said "such as", so the list is explicitly partial.
**Divine Spirit is now a trained spell** at level 30, and its duration **doubled to an hour**. **Improved Power Word: Fortitude was removed outright rather than made baseline** — worth stating precisely, because "the Fortitude tax is gone" is true while "Fortitude became baseline" is not what any source says. **Lightwell was de-talented rather than deleted** — still trained at 40.
Two further de-taxes are racial rather than talent: **Devouring Plague and Fear Ward are now universal to all Priests.** Fear Ward paid for it — **duration cut from 10 minutes to 3, with a 3-minute cooldown.**
One stacking conflict this creates: the camping **Faction Banner** grants Spirit and is exclusive with Divine Spirit, which now matters to *every* Priest.
## Discipline *(demo)*
The design thrust is stated plainly: rather than raising raw healing, the additions give Discipline "more utility, damage, and damage prevention".
| New talent | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Penance** | Channelled Holy volley that either damages an enemy or heals an ally |
| **Divine Aegis** | Critical heals leave an absorb shield |
| **Renewed Hope** | Direct heals gain crit against Weakened Soul targets, and cut its duration |
| **Soul Warding** | Power Word: Shield cooldown −4s, cost −15% |
| **Twin Disciplines** | Damage and healing of instant casts |
| **Power in Light** | Smite and Penance hit harder into Holy Fire |
| **Holy Precision** | +18% Holy spell hit at 3 ranks |
Removed: Unbreakable Will, Improved Power Word: Fortitude, Force of Will, Divine Spirit.
**Wand Specialization went from 5 ranks to 2 and from 5% to 13%** — the only hard numbers in the entire demo talent list. **Meditation was massively buffed to 50%** at 3 ranks. **Power Infusion now requires Penance**, coupling Discipline's 31-point to its 21-point for the first time, and it does **not** grant haste.
The intended shape at 60 is a Power Word: Shield uptime engine built on Penance, Soul Warding and Renewed Hope. That is design intent, not a tested rotation.
## Holy *(demo)*
Removed: Improved Prayer of Healing and **Lightwell**, vanilla's 31-point capstone — which is now **Prayer of Mending**.
New: **Prayer of Mending** fires when the target takes damage *or receives non-periodic healing*, then jumps, **up to five times**. **Binding Heal** heals target and Priest simultaneously at low threat. And **Litany of Light** restores mana when you cast a healing spell **different from your previous one** — an explicit anti-spam mechanic, and the biggest change to how Holy plays.
**Healing Focus is renamed Twilight Focus** and now protects *any* spell, not just heals. **Spirit of Redemption** makes every healing spell free and lasts 15 seconds rather than 10.
**Prayer of Healing was reworked and heavily nerfed** — roughly a 38% throughput cut *plus* a targeting change: it now heals a **chosen target's** party at 40 yards, rather than your own party at 30.
## Shadow *(demo)*
**Shadow Weaving was redesigned and it is the most consequential change in the tree.** It no longer debuffs the target's Shadow damage taken — it now increases **the Priest's own Shadow damage, stacking to five**. That un-couples it from raid stacking, from other Shadow Priests, **and from Warlock synergy**, which the community resents.
**Shadowform was rebuilt:** +10% Shadow damage, **−50% Shadow mana cost**, +100% Shadow crit damage bonus, −15% physical damage taken. And the lockout changed from "cannot cast Holy spells" to "cannot cast **healing** spells" — a real widening, since **Smite, Holy Fire and Resurrection become legal in Shadowform**.
**Shadow Word: Death is a new baseline spell** with a clause nobody's coverage mentions: *"If your target is not killed by Shadow Word: Death, you take backlash damage equal to 10% of your maximum health."* **Silence now interrupts an active cast** as well as locking out; in 1.12 it did not interrupt.
**There is no snapshotting in Forever.** Every vanilla DoT-snapshotting habit is dead.
## Rotation at 20
The beta cap is 20, so every Priest rotation in existence is a level-20 rotation — and **the wand is a load-bearing rotational button** in all three specs.
**Damage filler, all specs:** Fortitude and Inner Fire, Power Word: Shield before the pull, Holy Fire *(Discipline)*, Smite as it closes, Mind Blast, Shadow Word: Pain, then wand it down. At 20 **Shadow opens with Smite and has no Mind Flay step**.
**Healing:** Power Word: Shield to buy cast time, Heal for throughput, Renew for chip damage or while moving.
The stated failure mode is worth quoting: *"The main reason you'll die while playing as a Priest is running out of mana while still fighting against a healthy foe."*
**But the wand advice is already stale.** The 24 September build shipped *"wands no longer gain any additional damage from the user's spell damage"*, and Blizzard has a further wand nerf queued. Any guide recommending wand-weaving as the mana solution needs that caveat.
**There is no level-60 rotation from any source, and we will not construct one.**
## Stats
**The five-second rule survives intact**, and Forever's version has confirmed detail:
- It keys on cast **completion** — cancelling or being interrupted does not start the lockout.
- **MP5 ignores the rule entirely**, which is why it sits second for healers rather than third.
- Bypasses are Meditation and Spirit Tap, and Forever's Spirit Tap can now carry regeneration **through casting**.
- **Downranking is still live and still advised.**
**Discipline and Holy:** Healing Power first — and the reasoning is *mana*, not throughput, since casting less often to heal the same amount frees GCDs. Then MP5, Spirit, Intellect, and Critical Strike, which is called "an amazing stat" for **Discipline** specifically because it drives Renewed Hope and Divine Aegis.
**Shadow:** Spell Power, Crit, Hit, Intellect, Stamina, MP5. Hit is deliberately deprioritised *at 20*, with the note that it takes centre stage at endgame. Since hit is now one unified stat, **that endgame hit requirement is the Priest's biggest open gearing question.**
## Race
**Priest races: Human, Dwarf, Night Elf, Gnome, Undead, Troll.** Not Orc, not Tauren, and **not Skyborne**.
| Ability | Forever status |
| --- | --- |
| Devouring Plague | **Universal** to all Priests |
| Fear Ward | **Universal**, but cut to 3 minutes |
| Desperate Prayer | **Dwarf keeps it, Human loses it** for new **Divine Grace** |
| Chastise | **New, Dwarf only** — a Humanoid soft-CC that immobilises |
| Elune's Grace | **Reworked** — now covers melee *and* ranged, dodge 10% → **50%** |
| Starshards | Damage nearly doubled, **gained a 30-second cooldown** |
| Touch of Weakness / Hex of Weakness | Kept, both rewritten to reduce **melee attack power** |
| Symbol of Hope | **Not present**, and a false premise — it is a TBC Draenei ability |
**New Undead racial, Dark Sacrifice:** trades your own health over 15 seconds for mana, scaling with Spirit. With **Cannibalize now restoring mana as well as health**, Undead gets a real two-minute mana cooldown — which is why every recommendation puts Undead first on Horde.
**New Human racial, Divine Grace:** heals a target below 50% health *and removes Weakened Soul from them*. That second clause is a Power Word: Shield reset.
**Gnome Priest is the new combo, and it is contentious.** **Confounding Flash** confuses up to five enemies; **Contingency Plan** wards an ally and pays out when they drop below 35% health. It is compared to retail's Guardian Spirit, and Icy Veins expects it to be reworked, noting the Gnome racials "are causing an uproar". One careful counter-reading: **Contingency Plan is not a death save** — it pays out only after the target has already fallen that low.
**Weapon-skill racials became caster racials.** A **Human Priest wielding a sword gets +2% spell crit.**
Picks, all provisional: **Gnome** overall on Alliance, with Human a genuine toss-up for Discipline; **Night Elf** for Alliance Shadow, because Elune's Light is a crit burst window made more valuable by the absence of snapshotting; **Undead** for every Horde spec.
## Still unknown
Which talents occupy the 16-point row — derivations disagree, and one client read suggests Shadow may have **none**. Contingency Plan's max-rank values, which the client does not ship. Almost every Priest spell value: Greater Heal, Flash Heal, Prayer of Healing, Mind Blast, Holy Nova, Dispel, Inner Fire, Shackle Undead, Psychic Scream and Resurrection have **no published changes and no published confirmations of non-change**. Mass Dispel most likely does not exist, as it was never a 1.12 ability.
