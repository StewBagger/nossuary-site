Nine class guides for World of Warcraft: Forever — written for players who have never touched WoW as well as for people coming back to it.
## New to World of Warcraft?
Start here. Forever is a fresh start of the original 1–60 game, which means **everyone is new on 4 November**, including the people who have played for twenty years. There is no back catalogue to catch up on and nothing to unlock first.
Each guide below opens with a **Starting out** section written for someone who has never played: what the class is, what its specs actually do, how its resource works, and what you press in your first twenty levels. The rest of each page is for people who played before and want to know what changed. **Read the first part, ignore the second, and come back to it later.**
### The three roles
Group content in this game is built around three jobs. You do not have to pick one now, but knowing the words helps.
| Role | What you actually do |
| --- | --- |
| **Tank** | Hold the monsters' attention so they hit you instead of everyone else, and survive it. One per group. |
| **Healer** | Keep everyone alive, mostly by watching health bars rather than the fight. One per group. |
| **DPS** | Deal damage and kill things. Three per five-person group, so this is the most common role and the easiest to find a slot in — and the one with the longest queue when you want a group. |
Some classes can do only one of these. Some can do all three, though not at the same time — you swap by respending your talent points and changing gear.
### Which class should you pick?
Honestly: the one whose fantasy appeals to you. At this level of the game every class can finish the levelling content solo, and the differences below matter far more to how it *feels* than to whether you succeed.
| If you want to... | Consider |
| --- | --- |
| Be hard to kill and hold the line | Warrior, Paladin, Druid |
| Keep people alive | Priest, Paladin, Shaman, Druid |
| Kill things from a distance | Mage, Warlock, Hunter, Shaman, Priest, Druid |
| Kill things up close | Rogue, Warrior, Paladin, Shaman, Hunter, Druid |
| Level alone with the least friction | Hunter, Warlock |
| Always be able to find a group | Priest, Paladin, Druid, Warrior |
| Have the most buttons and the most to learn | Druid, Shaman, Paladin |
Two honest warnings. **Warrior is the slowest and most frustrating class to level** and only becomes strong with good gear — it is a wonderful tank and a rough first character. And **Hunter is by a distance the easiest class to level and one of the hardest to play well in a group**, because the pet does a lot of your thinking for you until suddenly it cannot.
### What to expect
Levelling from 1 to 60 takes a long time by modern standards — this is deliberately the pace of the original game. You will walk a great deal before your mount. You will need to stop and drink after fights if you use mana. Dying costs you time rather than progress.
Group content means five-person dungeons, and there is **no automatic group finder** — you find people by asking, which is slower and considerably more social than modern WoW. Raids do not open until 9 December, five weeks after launch.
## If you played WoW before
The second half of each guide is the diff: what Forever changed against vanilla 1.12, tagged by how well each claim is sourced.
Forever launches **4 November 2026** and its first raids open **9 December**. Blizzard is running no raid PTR, the later dungeons and raids are encrypted, and **item stats are obfuscated until an item drops for you or someone on your realm**. The beta runs to 21 October and its level cap is **still 20** — a rise to 30 was announced but never dated.
**So every class guide in existence, including these, is a level-20 guide.** Any site showing you a level-60 best-in-slot list or a tank tier list for Forever has invented it. We would rather tell you that than fill the gap.
### The changes that touch every class
- **Hit is one stat.** Melee, ranged and spell hit are merged, and so is crit. From the client's own combat-ratings table: **10 rating per 1% hit, 14 per 1% crit, 10 per 1% haste and expertise**, and Defense converts at 1 because it is still a skill.
- **Talent trees kept their vanilla shape** but gained a **16-point milestone** alongside the old 11, 21 and 31.
- **There is no DoT or HoT snapshotting.** Stats update dynamically, which kills every vanilla snapshotting habit. **Periodic damage can crit.**
- **Weapon skill still works but items carry far less of it**, and no weapon-skill racials remain — they all became crit.
- **Expertise is new**, and vanishingly rare: exactly one item in a 2,797-item catalogue carries it.
- **Haste exists but no gear has it.** The only sources are racials and one PTR-flagged enchant.
Three specs tank: Protection Warrior, Protection Paladin and Feral Druid in bear form. Shaman is widely said to be ruled out — and that claim has **no Blizzard source**, which the Shaman guide goes into.
### A word on sources
The beta has had **exactly one balance pass**, on 24 September. Every build since reads "talents, spells and items did not change". That makes this data unusually stable and also barely tuned — a launch-day pass could invalidate a good deal of it. Re-read before you commit to a main.
