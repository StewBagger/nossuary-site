Levelling guides for all nine classes in World of Warcraft: Forever, which relaunches the 1–60 game on **November 4, 2026**, with everyone starting from scratch on the same day.

## Everyone is new on November 4
There is no back catalogue to catch up on, nothing to unlock first and no established players with a head start. If you have never played WoW, this is the single best moment in twenty years to start.
Each class guide below is written for levelling from 1 to 60, opens with a short section for players new to that class, and says plainly where something is unknown rather than guessing.
## The three roles
Group content is built around three jobs. You do not have to choose now, but the words come up constantly.
| Role | What you actually do | Per five-person group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tank** | Hold the monsters' attention so they hit you instead of everyone else, and survive it | One |
| **Healer** | Keep everyone alive, mostly by watching health bars rather than the fight | One |
| **DPS** | Deal damage and kill things | Three |
Some classes do only one of these. Some do all three, though never at once — you switch by respending talent points and changing gear.
Because groups need three DPS and only one of each other role, **DPS is the most common thing to be and the hardest to get invited as.** Tanks and healers get invited instantly. There is no automatic group finder in Forever, so grouping means asking.
## "Best for levelling" almost always means "best for soloing"
**This is the most important thing on the page, and every guide site gets it wrong by omission.**
When a guide rates a spec for levelling, it is nearly always rating **how fast it kills things by itself.** That is a real question, and it is not the only one. **Levelling in groups and dungeons is a different activity**, and the specs that rate worst for soloing, healers especially, are often the ones that reach 60 with the least waiting, because a healer never sits in a queue.
Forever sharpened that distinction by paying dungeon *quest* experience above normal quest values — then **pulled half of that back on October 1**, when Blizzard cut the extra experience from dungeon quests by 50%, saying directly that it was responding to feedback on levelling speed. A dungeon route is still a deliberate design rather than a workaround; it is simply worth less than it was before that build, and this is the number most likely to move again before launch.
So when a page below says a spec is *slow*, read it as **slow to kill things alone**. Every spec in the game can reach level 60. None of them is a trap.
**Where it matters most:**
| If you mostly | Then |
| --- | --- |
| Quest alone | Take the solo recommendation. It is measuring the right thing |
| Run dungeons | Healers and tanks are among the fastest, not the slowest. Ignore the low levelling scores |
| Play with a friend | Almost anything works. Pick what you want to play at 60 |

## Which class should you pick?
Honestly, the one whose fantasy appeals. Every class finishes the levelling game solo, and the differences below change how it *feels* far more than whether you succeed.
| If you want to… | Consider |
| --- | --- |
| Be hard to kill and hold the line | Warrior, Paladin, Druid |
| Keep people alive | Priest, Paladin, Shaman, Druid |
| Kill things from a distance | Mage, Warlock, Hunter, Shaman, Priest, Druid |
| Kill things up close | Rogue, Warrior, Paladin, Shaman, Druid |
| Level alone with the least friction | Hunter, Warlock |
| Be wanted for groups from day one | Priest, Paladin, Druid, Warrior |
| Have the most to learn | Druid, Shaman, Paladin, Warlock |
Three honest warnings. **Warrior is among the harder classes to level** and only becomes strong with good gear — a superb tank and a rough first character. The reputation is narrower than it was: Blizzard concedes Rage-generation bugs it is still tracking down, a critical strike now gives **100% increased Rage**, and Icy Veins scores Protection above both Arms and Fury for levelling. **Hunter is by a distance the easiest to level and one of the hardest to play well in a group**, because the pet covers your mistakes until it suddenly cannot. And **Hunter is a ranged class for most of the levelling game** — its melee payoff is bought with talent points rather than trained, `Counterattack` at the new 16-point milestone and `Strider Kick` at 21, and both fit inside the talent budget at the level-30 cap.
## Professions
**[Profession guides are here](/guides/wow-forever/professions/)**: all twelve, plus the two systems Forever added.
Three things worth knowing before you pick:
- **Take Cooking at level 1.** 103 of its dishes give **+5% experience from kills**, and the first one works at skill 1 on a level-1 character. It costs you no primary slot.
- **Only the six crafting professions earn Legacy Points.** None of the three gathering professions earns any of the 18 on offer, so a gathering pair earns you nothing.
- **Camping is new**, and it lets a group hand itself class buffs it does not have.
## What is new in Forever
Beyond the new zones and dungeons, these change how you level whatever you pick. The first three moved in the **October 1** build, so they are what a guide written last week has wrong.
- **Dungeons pay in quests, not in kills.** Dungeon *quest* experience is paid above normal quest values — then cut by 50% on October 1, so that bonus is now half what it was. A matching cut to kill experience inside dungeons is widely repeated, and **Blizzard has published no number for it**: that is the client reading, not an official figure. Run each dungeon once for its quests rather than repeating it, and expect the quest number to keep moving: Blizzard is openly tuning levelling speed during the beta.
- **Fourteen dungeons are open at the level-30 cap**, and three of them arrived on October 1: **Excavation Site: Wetlands** (26–31), **Razorfen Downs** (25+) and **Uldaman** (30+), alongside the eleven already available: Ragefire Chasm, Hall of Thanes, Ruins of Lordaeron, Wailing Caverns, the Deadmines, Shadowfang Keep, the Stockade, Blackfathom Deeps, Gnomeregan, Razorfen Kraul and Scarlet Monastery.
- **A low-rank spell is no longer a free efficiency trick.** Casting a rank well below your level now gets less from your Spell Damage and Spell Healing *and* has a reduced chance to trigger your own talents. Blizzard's example: a rank 1 Frostbolt at level 60 has a **0%** chance to proc `Frostbite`. Downranking to save mana now costs you procs, so it is a decision rather than a habit.
- **Talent points can eventually start earlier than 10. But not on your first character.** The account-wide Legacy system has a perk, `Talented`: five ranks, each moving your first talent point one level earlier, from 10 down to 5. It costs **ten Legacy Points** — five ranks of `Well Rested` first, then five of its own, and Legacy Points are earned one per challenge, such as levelling a class to 25, 45 or 60, or a profession to 150, 225 or 300. **A brand-new account on November 4 has none.** The 51-point total at 60 never changes; the perk only moves when the points arrive, and at full rank a level-30 character holds 26 points instead of 21.
- **Six new race and class combinations**: Gnome Priest, Human Hunter, Dwarf Shaman, Orc Mage, Troll Warlock and Undead Paladin. **Paladin and Shaman are no longer faction-locked.** Blizzard says more combinations are planned.
- **There is a tenth race.** The **Skyborne** are neutral, playable on either faction, with their own starting area in the Zephras Isles, and they play **Druid, Hunter, Rogue and Warrior on both sides, Mage on Alliance only and Shaman on Horde only.** The race and its starting experience need an optional upgrade purchase, so this is the one class-and-race choice on this page that costs money.
- **Every race now carries four racials**, two active and two passive, and the familiar names often do something different. Do not pick from a Classic racial ranking.
- **Every one of the 27 talent trees gained a fourth one-point ability at 16 points**, alongside the existing 11, 21 and 31-point talents. Talents that merely improved a class buff, such as Blessing of Kings and Improved Mark of the Wild, were removed, because those improvements are baseline now. Divine Spirit went the same way for a different reason: it was the buff itself rather than an improvement to one, and it is now baseline to every Priest, learned at level 30.
- **Dual Specialization arrives at level 40**, so from 40 you carry two talent builds and swap between them instead of paying to respec. Blizzard states this in both class deep dives. For a hybrid — a Druid, Paladin, Priest, Shaman or Warrior — it is the level the class stops being one job at a time.
- **Talents are changed at a class trainer in a major city**, Legacy talents included. There is no respec-anywhere.
- **Hit and critical strike are combined across melee, ranged and spells**, and healing gear also grants some bonus damage. One stat line now serves a whole character, which is why weapon and gear advice on these pages differs from Classic's.
- **Weapon-specialisation racials became critical-strike racials**, and they work off an equipped weapon rather than one you swing, so weapon type is now worth a thought even for casters.
The levelling pace itself is unchanged from Classic. Expect to walk a great deal before your mount, to drink after fights if you use mana, and for dying to cost time rather than progress.
