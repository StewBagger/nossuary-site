## Where the verdicts come from
Every evaluative claim on these pages belongs to someone. Spec and class verdicts are read off the three guide sites: Wowhead, Icy Veins and ClassicWoW.gg. They are also read off Blizzard's own posts, which outrank all three wherever they say anything. Where a page reports a video creator's judgement, it names the creator and links the video, and the line is never a verdict of ours. Where those sources agree, we say so. Where they disagree, the page names which said what rather than quietly picking a winner. Where only one of them scores something, we say that too. Where we worked something out ourselves, such as an extended talent build or a judgement no source makes, it carries the marker *(ours, not a source's)*. We do not issue our own verdict and leave it reading like a fact. We **do not rank the specs** for you at all. A spec is introduced by what it is for and what it is strongest at, because best and worst depend on whether you are questing alone or standing in a group.
Mechanics are different, and they are stated flatly: talent trees, training levels and numbers come from the launch client, its hotfixes and Blizzard directly, not from anyone's opinion.
## Everyone is new on November 4
There is no back catalogue to catch up on, nothing to unlock first and no established players with a head start. If you have never played WoW, our own view is that there has not been a better moment in twenty years to start.
Each class guide below is written for levelling from 1 to 60, opens with a short section for players new to that class, and says plainly where something is unknown rather than guessing.
## The three roles
Group content is built around three jobs. You do not have to choose now, but the words come up constantly.
| Role | What you actually do | Per five-person group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tank** | Hold the monsters' attention so they hit you instead of everyone else, and survive it | One |
| **Healer** | Keep everyone alive, mostly by watching health bars rather than the fight | One |
| **DPS** | Deal damage and kill things | Three |
Some classes do only one of these. Some do all three, though never at once — you switch by respending talent points and changing gear.
Because groups need three DPS and only one of each other role, **DPS is the most common role and the hardest to get invited as**. Tanks and healers get invited instantly *(ours, not a source's)*. Forever has a Group Finder for posting and browsing groups, with a dungeon list, but it does not form the group for you, so grouping still means asking.
## "Best for levelling" almost always means "best for soloing"
**Every guide site gets this wrong by omission** *(ours, not a source's)*, and it matters more than anything else here.
When a guide rates a spec for levelling, it is nearly always rating **how fast it kills things by itself**. That matters, but it is not the only question. Levelling in groups and dungeons is a different activity. The specs that rate worst for soloing, healers especially, often reach 60 with the least waiting *(ours, not a source's)*, because a healer never sits in a queue.
Forever sharpened that distinction by paying dungeon *quest* experience above normal quest values, and Blizzard raised the experience from dungeon kills by about 20% on October 8. A dungeon route is a deliberate design rather than a workaround.
The pages below therefore do not rank the specs against each other. Each one says what its spec is for and what it is strongest at. The guide sites' levelling scores are still reported, on the spec pages where there is room to say what they measure: solo kill speed, and nothing else. Every spec in the game can reach level 60. None of them is a trap.
Where it matters most:
| If you mostly | Then |
| --- | --- |
| Quest alone | The levelling scores on the spec pages are measuring the right thing for you |
| Run dungeons | Ignore those scores. Healers and tanks wait least for a group |
| Play with a friend | Almost anything works. Pick what you want to play at 60 |

## Which class should you pick?
Honestly, the one whose fantasy appeals. Every class finishes the levelling game solo, and the differences below change how it *feels* far more than whether you succeed. **No source ranked the nine classes against each other for Forever when we last checked in October**: Icy Veins' tier lists all read *"COMING SOON"*. So the rows below are role facts, which class can do which job, and nothing more.
| If you want to… | Consider |
| --- | --- |
| Be hard to kill and hold the line | Warrior, Paladin, Druid |
| Keep people alive | Priest, Paladin, Shaman, Druid |
| Kill things from a distance | Mage, Warlock, Hunter, Shaman, Priest, Druid |
| Kill things up close | Rogue, Warrior, Paladin, Shaman, Druid, Hunter (Survival) |
Two things before you commit. **Warrior's Rage is not Classic's:** Blizzard balanced it for a questing Warrior's armour rather than a tank's and fixed its Rage-from-damage bugs on October 8, and a critical strike gives 100% increased Rage. Hunter is a ranged class for most of the levelling game. Its melee payoff is bought with talent points rather than trained: `Counterattack` at the new 16-point milestone and `Strider Kick` at 21. Both fit inside the points a level-30 character holds.
## Professions
**[Profession guides are here](/guides/wow-forever/professions/)**: all twelve, plus the two systems Forever added.
Three things before you pick:
- **Take Cooking at level 1.** 103 of its dishes give +5% experience from kills, and the first one works at skill 1 on a level-1 character. It costs you no primary slot.
- **Only the six crafting professions earn Legacy Points.** None of the three gathering professions earns any of the 18 on offer, so a gathering pair earns you nothing.
- **Camping is new**, and it lets a group hand itself class buffs it does not have.
## What is new in Forever
Beyond the new zones and dungeons, these change how you level whatever you pick.
- **Dungeons pay in quests and in kills.** Dungeon *quests* pay experience above normal quest values, and Blizzard raised the experience from dungeon kills by about 20% on October 8. Each party member earns a kill's experience by their own level, so a higher-level friend does not shrink your share, unless they are so far above the enemy that nobody in the party earns any.
- **Nine new dungeons join the Classic list**: Hall of Thanes, Ruins of Lordaeron, Excavation Site: Wetlands (26–31), the City of Dalaran, the Drowned City, Krol'dok Stronghold, Alcaz Prison, Blackmaw Hold and the Shaper's Terrace.
- **A low-rank spell is no longer a free efficiency trick.** Casting a rank well below your level gets less from your Spell Damage and Spell Healing. Blizzard's November 2 notes take the low-rank penalty off various talents' chance to trigger, and keep it on one spell: a Mage's `Blizzard`, cast at a rank learned more than 12 levels below your level, has a reduced chance to trigger `Arcane Concentration`, `Frostbite` and `Fingers of Frost`.
- **Talent points can eventually start earlier than 10, but not on your first character.** The account-wide Legacy system has a perk, `Talented`: five ranks, each moving your first talent point one level earlier, from 10 down to 5. It costs ten Legacy Points, and you need five ranks of `Well Rested` first, then five of its own. Legacy Points are earned one per challenge, such as levelling a class to 25, 45 or 60, or a profession to 150, 225 or 300. A brand-new account on November 4 has none. The 51-point total at 60 never changes: the perk only moves when the points arrive *(ours, not a source's)*, and at full rank a level-30 character holds 26 points instead of 21.
- **Six new race and class combinations**: Gnome Priest, Human Hunter, Dwarf Shaman, Orc Mage, Troll Warlock and Undead Paladin. Paladin and Shaman are no longer faction-locked, and Blizzard says more combinations are planned.
- **There is a ninth race.** The Skyborne choose a faction at character creation, with their own starting area on Zephras Isle. They play Druid, Hunter, Rogue and Warrior on both sides, Mage on Alliance only and Shaman on Horde only. The race and its starting experience need an optional upgrade purchase, so this is the one class-and-race choice here that costs money.
- **Every race now carries four racials**, two active and two passive, and the familiar names often do something different. Blizzard's final pass over them is in the November 2 notes, so do not pick from a Classic racial ranking or a Forever one written before that. The same notes give every race its correct base stats: a Tauren Warrior starts with 5 more Strength than a Human Warrior.
- **Every one of the 27 talent trees gained a fourth one-point ability at 16 points**, alongside the existing 11, 21 and 31-point talents. Talents that merely improved a class buff, such as Blessing of Kings and Improved Mark of the Wild, were removed, because those improvements are baseline now. Divine Spirit went the same way for a different reason: it was the buff itself rather than an improvement to one, and it is now baseline to every Priest, learned at level 30.
- **Dual Specialization arrives at level 40**, so from 40 you carry two talent builds and swap between them instead of paying to respec. Blizzard states this in every class deep dive. For a hybrid Druid, Paladin, Priest, Shaman or Warrior, it is the level the class stops being one job at a time.
- Talents are changed at a class trainer in a major city, Legacy talents included. There is no respec-anywhere.
- **Hit and critical strike are combined across melee, ranged and spells**, and healing gear also grants some bonus damage. One stat line now serves a whole character, so weapon and gear advice on these pages differs from Classic's.
- **Classes deal more damage, and higher-level monsters have more health to match.** Blizzard's November 2 notes raise damage for classes across the game and raise the health of higher-level creatures to compensate. Blizzard plans no big tuning changes before the raids open, apart from bugs and serious outliers.
- **Training every class ability from 1 to 60 costs between 206 and 207 gold**, whichever class you play. Most abilities learned at the same level cost the same to train, except the Priest's race-specific ones, and Warlock costs are lower by what Blizzard expects you to spend on Grimoires for your pets.
- **New spells do not go on your action bars by themselves.** After each trainer visit, open the spellbook and drag them on.
- **Wands and Auto Shot need you to stand still for half a second before they fire**, as in early World of Warcraft, and Blizzard's notes say wands gain no extra damage from your Spell Damage. Stop moving before you start one.
- **Roots that come from procs have diminishing returns**: `Frostbite`, `Entrapment`, `Improved Wing Clip`, `Counterattack` and `Improved Hamstring` all count.
- **Monsters respawn fastest in the starting zones** and more slowly as you level, so that launch crowds do not hit hard bottlenecks. Blizzard says it will slow the low-level zones to more typical rates once the launch surge settles.
- **Bags do not equip themselves.** A new bag goes into your inventory, and you drag it into an empty bag slot.
- **Weapon type no longer matters to any racial.** The old weapon specialisations are gone. Human `Ruthless` is +1% crit with all spells and attacks, Dwarf has `Artisan's Precision` for +1% hit, and Orc has `Command` for +5% Hunter and Warlock pet damage. Choose weapons on their stats.
Expect a Classic-paced climb *(ours, not a source's)*: a great deal of walking before your mount, drinking after fights if you use mana, and dying costing time rather than progress.
