Guides to all nine classes in World of Warcraft: Forever, the 1–60 game that launched on **November 4, 2026**. Each class page covers the whole climb to 60 and what is published for level 60 so far.
:::scope
These pages describe the live game on client 1.60.1.70938, with Blizzard's hotfixes through November 12. Mechanics come from Blizzard's posts and the game client: talent trees, training levels and spell numbers. Blizzard's posts rank first, and its nine class deep dives are complete. Where a tooltip on a class page differs from the prose, trust the prose: a tooltip can lag a hotfix.
Builds, rotations and stat priorities come from Wowhead, Icy Veins and ClassicWoW.gg, named against each other where they disagree. Wowhead and Icy Veins now publish level-60 pages for every spec. ClassicWoW.gg has not updated a Forever page since October 14, so its text is from the beta.
What is still thin: **no raid is open until December 9**, so nobody has a raid log, and every level-60 build and best-in-slot list in print is a prediction. We could not read combat logs for this pass. Blizzard tunes classes on Tuesdays, so a number on these pages can change within a week.

## Where the verdicts come from
Every evaluative claim on these pages belongs to someone. Spec and class verdicts are read off the three guide sites: Wowhead, Icy Veins and ClassicWoW.gg. They are also read off Blizzard's own posts, which outrank all three wherever they say anything. A guide page written before a Blizzard change does not outrank that change, so each class page checks the date of the page it quotes. Where a page reports a video creator's judgement, it names the creator and links the video, and the line is never a verdict of ours. Where those sources agree, we say so. Where they disagree, the page names which said what rather than quietly picking a winner. Where only one of them scores something, we say that too. Where we worked something out ourselves, such as an extended talent build or a judgement no source makes, it carries the marker *(ours, not a source's)*. We do not issue our own verdict and leave it reading like a fact. We **do not rank the specs** for you at all. A spec is introduced by what it is for and what it is strongest at, because best and worst depend on whether you are questing alone or standing in a group.
Mechanics are different, and they are stated flatly: talent trees, training levels and numbers come from the client, its hotfixes and Blizzard directly, not from anyone's opinion.

## The live game since launch
Blizzard has posted hotfix notes dated November 5, 6, 9, 10 and 11, with a further set live on November 12. These are the changes that cross class lines.
- **Class tuning arrives on Tuesdays.** Blizzard's November 2 notes said it planned no big tuning before the raids, apart from bugs and serious outliers. Four days later it announced the first round, shipped it on November 10, and said it would make *"class balance changes on Tuesday, and then on future Tuesdays if necessary"*. The first round changed Hunter pets, Warlock pets and `Wrack`, and Enhancement Shaman. Each class page carries its own changes.
- **Dungeon kill experience is 60% of Classic Era's.** It launched at 40%. Blizzard raised it by half on November 8 and said two days later that the new level *"is working well"*. Dungeon *quests* still pay above normal quest values.
- **Quests handed in at 60 do not convert experience into gold.** Blizzard removed that conversion on purpose, so there is no reason to hoard finished quests for level 60. On November 10 it added gold to most quests of level 48 and higher, including quests that never paid gold before.
- **The PvP rulesets limit character creation on the larger faction.** Blizzard is aiming at an even split per region. On November 10 the limited side was Horde on North American PvP and Alliance on EU Roleplay-PvP, and Blizzard said that can flip.
- **Zone population caps were lowered** on November 9 in congested zones, more so on the PvP rulesets.
- **Racials were hotfixed three times.** They are listed under [Racials](#racials) below.
## The three roles
Group content is built around three jobs. You do not have to choose now, but the words come up constantly.
| Role | What you actually do | Per five-person group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tank** | Hold the monsters' attention so they hit you instead of everyone else, and survive it | One |
| **Healer** | Keep everyone alive, mostly by watching health bars rather than the fight | One |
| **DPS** | Deal damage and kill things | Three |
Some classes do only one of these. Some do all three, though never at once. From level 40 Dual Specialization lets one character hold two of them.
Because groups need three DPS and only one of each other role, **DPS is the most common role and the hardest to get invited as**. Tanks and healers get invited instantly *(ours, not a source's)*. Forever has a Group Finder for posting and browsing groups, with a dungeon list, but it does not form the group for you, so grouping still means asking.
## "Best for levelling" almost always means "best for soloing"
**Every guide site gets this wrong by omission** *(ours, not a source's)*, and it matters more than anything else here.
When a guide rates a spec for levelling, it is nearly always rating **how fast it kills things by itself**. That matters, but it is not the only question. Levelling in groups and dungeons is a different activity. The specs that rate worst for soloing, healers especially, find groups with the least waiting *(ours, not a source's)*.
Do not read that as "dungeons are the fast route". Dungeon kills pay 60% of what they pay in Classic Era, and Blizzard has said it wants dungeon levelling speed to *"fit with the rest of WoW Forever content"*. Dungeon quests pay above normal quest values, so the first run of each dungeon is worth more than the repeats *(ours, not a source's)*.
The pages below therefore do not rank the specs against each other. Each one says what its spec is for and what it is strongest at. The guide sites' levelling scores are still reported, on the spec pages where there is room to say what they measure: solo kill speed, and nothing else. Every spec in the game can reach level 60. None of them is a trap.
Where it matters most:
| If you mostly | Then |
| --- | --- |
| Quest alone | The levelling scores on the spec pages are measuring the right thing for you |
| Run dungeons | Ignore those scores. Healers and tanks wait least for a group |
| Play with a friend | Almost anything works. Pick what you want to play at 60 |

## Which class should you pick?
Honestly, the one whose fantasy appeals. Every class finishes the levelling game solo, and the differences below change how it *feels* far more than whether you succeed. The class rankings in print are the predictions described above, so the rows below are role facts, which class can do which job, and nothing more.
| If you want to… | Consider |
| --- | --- |
| Be hard to kill and hold the line | Warrior, Paladin, Druid |
| Keep people alive | Priest, Paladin, Shaman, Druid |
| Kill things from a distance | Mage, Warlock, Hunter, Shaman, Priest, Druid |
| Kill things up close | Rogue, Warrior, Paladin, Shaman, Druid, Hunter (Survival) |
Two things before you commit. **Warrior's Rage is not Classic's:** Blizzard balanced it for a questing Warrior's armour rather than a tank's, and a critical strike gives 100% increased Rage. Hunter is a ranged class for most of the levelling game. Its melee payoff is bought with talent points rather than trained: `Counterattack` at the 16-point milestone and `Strider Kick` at 21, both in Survival.
## Before you create a character
- **Agree on ruleset and faction with your friends first.** You pick a ruleset, not a realm: Normal, PvP, Roleplay or Roleplay-PvP. Characters group, join guilds, run dungeons and raid only within the same ruleset and faction. On a PvP ruleset an account plays one faction.
- **The faction you want may be closed.** The PvP rulesets limit creation on the larger faction, as described above.
- **Hardcore is not out.** Blizzard says the Hardcore ruleset comes some time after launch and has given no date.
- **Skyborne costs money.** The ninth race needs the Heroic Pack or a higher upgrade. The six new combinations on the original races do not.
## Racials
**Every race carries four racials**, two active and two passive, and the familiar names often do something different. Blizzard's last full pass is in the November 2 notes, and three hotfixes have followed. Do not pick from a Classic racial ranking or from a Forever one written before November 2.
- **Weapon type no longer matters to any racial.** The old weapon specialisations are gone, because Blizzard did not want loot judged by *"required"* weapon types. Choose weapons on their stats.
- **Human:** `Ruthless` is +1% critical strike chance with all spells and attacks. `The Human Spirit` is +10% Spirit.
- **Dwarf:** `Artisan's Precision` is +1% hit chance with spells and attacks.
- **Orc:** `Command` is +5% damage for Hunter and Warlock pets. On November 6 Blizzard fixed some Warlock pet abilities that were gaining too much from it. `Blood Fury` has a 2-minute cooldown.
- **Tauren:** `Plainsrunning` builds 2% movement speed a stack to 60%, and gives nothing while mounted. Since November 5 it cannot stack during a battleground's preparation phase, and its stacks are cancelled while you carry a flag. `Endurance` is +5% Health and nothing else. `Cultivation` now starts its cooldown only when the cast completes.
- **Troll:** `Berserking` lasts 15 seconds.
- **Undead:** `Touch of the Grave` has a 15-second internal cooldown.
- **Night Elf:** a Druid in `Shadowmeld` no longer gains access to `Ravage` and `Pounce`. Blizzard fixed that on November 6.
- **Skyborne:** `Wind Blessed` is +2% haste. The fourth racial depends on faction: `Read Leyline` for Alliance and `Skysight` for Horde. Blizzard's November 2 notes give `Skysight` 15% movement speed for 15 seconds. The client tooltip reads 30 seconds and adds mounted speed, and we could not check which one a player sees.
- **Base stats differ by race.** The November 2 notes corrected them: a Tauren Warrior starts with 5 more Strength than a Human Warrior.
Each class page lists the racials that matter for that class.
## Talent points and Dual Specialization
**A character earns 51 talent points, one per level from 10 to 60.** Blizzard says talents start at level 10. The 51-point cap is stated in the client's own text for the `Talented` Legacy perk, and Icy Veins' calculator and the WoWSims model both stop at 51.
One data file disagrees on its face. Raidbots' talent data lists 56 levels at which a point can arrive, 5 through 60. That is not 56 points. It is the two schedules laid over each other: levels 10 to 60 without `Talented`, and levels 5 to 55 with all five ranks of it *(our reading of the file, not a source's statement)*. Either way a character ends on 51.
A row opens when you have spent enough points in that tree. The levels below are the earliest possible, for a character that puts every point into one tree and has no `Talented` ranks *(arithmetic, ours)*.
| Row | Points already spent in the tree | Earliest level | One-point milestone talent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0 | 10 | |
| 2 | 5 | 15 | |
| 3 | 10 | 20 | The 11-point talent |
| 4 | 15 | 25 | The 16-point talent, new in Forever |
| 5 | 20 | 30 | The 21-point talent |
| 6 | 25 | 35 | |
| 7 | 30 | 40 | The 31-point talent |
- **51 points is one full tree and 20 more.** A 31-point talent leaves 20 points, which reaches row 4 of a second tree and its 16-point talent. Taking a second tree's 21-point talent means giving up the first tree's 31-point one *(arithmetic, ours)*.
- **Every one of the 27 trees has the 16-point talent.** Talents that merely improved a class buff, such as Blessing of Kings and Improved Mark of the Wild, were removed, because those improvements are baseline now. Divine Spirit is baseline to every Priest, learned at level 30.
- **Dual Specialization unlocks at level 40.** Blizzard states the level in every class deep dive. Wowhead reported on November 11 that it is bought from the class trainer for **50 gold**. Blizzard has not posted the price. Level 40 is also when you want a mount, so plan for both.
- **Talents are changed at a class trainer in a major city**, Legacy perks included. There is no respec-anywhere.
- **`Talented` moves the first point earlier, not the total higher.** It is a perk in the account-wide Legacy system with five ranks, each starting talent points one level sooner, from 9 down to 5. It sits behind five points spent in its tree, on `Well Rested` for example, so the full perk costs ten of the 16 Legacy Points a character can spend. With all five ranks the last talent point arrives at 55 instead of 60 *(arithmetic, ours)*.
## Professions
**[Profession guides are here](/guides/wow-forever/professions/)**: all twelve, plus the two systems Forever added.
Three things before you pick:
- **Take Cooking at level 1.** 103 of its dishes give +5% experience from kills, and the first one works at skill 1 on a level-1 character. It costs you no primary slot.
- **Only the six crafting professions earn Legacy Points.** None of the three gathering professions earns any of the 18 on offer, so a gathering pair earns you nothing.
- **Camping is new**, and it lets a group hand itself class buffs it does not have.
## Legacy
Legacy Points belong to the account. Each of the 65 challenges pays one point, once: a class reaching level 25, 45 or 60, a crafting profession reaching 150, 225 or 300, and dungeon, raid, PvP and exploration challenges. Each character spends the account's points again in its own trees, up to **16 at launch**. Blizzard says one character can reach that cap without alts. A new account starts with none.

## What else is different from Classic
- **A low-rank spell is no longer a free efficiency trick.** Casting a rank well below your level gets less from your Spell Damage and Spell Healing. Blizzard's November 2 notes take the low-rank penalty off various talents' chance to trigger, and keep it on one spell: a Mage's `Blizzard`, cast at a rank learned more than 12 levels below your level, has a reduced chance to trigger `Arcane Concentration`, `Frostbite` and `Fingers of Frost`.
- **Six new race and class combinations**: Gnome Priest, Human Hunter, Dwarf Shaman, Orc Mage, Troll Warlock and Undead Paladin. Paladin and Shaman are no longer faction-locked.
- **There is a ninth race.** The Skyborne choose a faction at character creation, with their own starting area on Zephras Isle for levels 1 to 12. They play Druid, Hunter, Rogue and Warrior on both sides, Mage on Alliance only and Shaman on Horde only.
- **Hit and critical strike are combined across melee, ranged and spells**, and bonus healing on gear also gives one third as much bonus damage. One stat line now serves a whole character, so weapon and gear advice on these pages differs from Classic's.
- **Classes deal more damage, and higher-level monsters have more health to match.** Blizzard's November 2 notes raised both.
- **Training every class ability from 1 to 60 costs between 206 and 207 gold**, whichever class you play. Most abilities learned at the same level cost the same to train, except the Priest's race-specific ones, and Warlock costs are lower by what Blizzard expects you to spend on Grimoires for your pets.
- **New spells do not go on your action bars by themselves.** After each trainer visit, open the spellbook and drag them on.
- **Wands and Auto Shot need you to stand still for half a second before they fire**, as in early World of Warcraft, and Blizzard's notes say wands gain no extra damage from your Spell Damage. Stop moving before you start one.
- **Roots that come from procs have diminishing returns**: `Frostbite`, `Entrapment`, `Improved Wing Clip`, `Counterattack` and `Improved Hamstring` all count.
- **Monsters respawn fastest in the starting zones** and more slowly as you level. Blizzard set that up for launch crowds and said it would slow the low-level zones to more typical rates once the surge settled. It has not announced that change yet.
- **Bags do not equip themselves.** A new bag goes into your inventory, and you drag it into an empty bag slot.
Expect a Classic-paced climb *(ours, not a source's)*: a great deal of walking before your mount, drinking after fights if you use mana, and dying costing time rather than progress.
