Misdirection does not exist, pets now inherit your stats, and Survival became a genuine melee spec. Hunter has 110 changes in the client diff — more than any other class.
## Starting out
Hunter is a ranged physical damage class that fights alongside a tamed beast. You shoot from a distance; the pet stands in front and holds the enemy's attention. That division of labour is the whole class, and it is why Hunter is the class most often recommended to someone who has never played WoW. You can kill things above your own level on your own, you spend little time recovering, and you will die less than most beginners do.
It is also the class with the widest gap between playing it and playing it well. Alone, the pet covers most of your mistakes. In a group the pet is not the tank, your footwork matters, and one careless shot pulls a second pack onto four other people. Easy to level, hard to be good at — both halves are true from the first hour.
Early on it feels slower than you expect, and fiddly at the edges. You need arrows or bullets, plus a quiver or ammo pouch to hold them, and both cost money and a bag slot. You also cannot shoot something standing on top of you: there is a minimum range, inside which your ranged attacks will not fire. That is deliberate, and every new Hunter meets it in confusion.
### What the specs do
| Spec | Role | In one line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beast Mastery | Ranged DPS | Your pet does a great deal of the work and survives almost anything. |
| Marksmanship | Ranged DPS | Your own shots do the work; the sharpest single-target shooting. |
| Survival | Melee DPS | Traps, control and mobility, fighting with a weapon in hand rather than a bow. |
You do not choose a spec at the start. You earn talent points as you level and spend them across the three trees, and a spec is only the name for where most of your points went. Your class trainer will unlearn the lot for a fee that rises each time you ask, so nothing is permanent. If you have never grouped: the tank holds the monster's attention and takes the hits, the healer keeps the tank and everyone else alive, and DPS — damage per second — kills the thing. Hunter is DPS in every spec.
### Your resource
You use mana, a blue bar that pays for every ability except your ordinary shot and swing. Mana does not refill from fighting. It creeps back while you stand still, comes back faster while you drink, and is spent far quicker than it returns. The habit that stops new Hunters running dry is to treat your ordinary auto-shot as the rotation and your abilities as decoration: an enemy dying to free shots costs nothing, and a full mana bar going into the next pull is worth more than a marginally faster kill.
Your pet has its own bar, focus, which it manages by itself. Focus regenerates on its own and pays for the pet's abilities, which it uses without being told. You never top it up. Forever changed how much of your pet's strength comes from your own gear; the pet section below has the detail.
Ammunition is a third resource in all but name. Buy stacks of it whenever you pass a vendor.
### Your first twenty levels
Auto Shot is your main source of damage and it keeps firing on its own. Learn to open a fight with it. Serpent Sting is a poison over time worth applying to anything that will live more than a few seconds. Hunter's Mark makes a target easier to hit and is the button most often forgotten entirely.
Around level ten you tame your first pet. Call Pet brings it out, Revive Pet brings it back when it dies, Mend Pet heals it during a fight, and Feed Pet keeps it content — an unhappy pet fights worse and will eventually leave. Taming and teaching a pet is a whole system the game explains poorly, so read the pet section properly rather than guessing.
Arcane Shot is instant damage for when you have spare mana. Aspects are stances you leave switched on: one for ranged damage, one for movement speed. Traps are placed on the ground before or during a fight to hold something still. Wing Clip and Concussive Shot slow a target that has closed on you, and they exist precisely to solve the minimum-range problem.
### Common beginner mistakes
- Running out of ammunition mid-fight, then finishing the fight in melee with a ranged weapon equipped.
- Standing still while a wounded enemy runs at you, instead of stepping back and slowing it.
- Sending the pet in and then attacking a different enemy, which gives you two fights at once.
- Never pressing Feed Pet, then wondering why the pet has become weak or has vanished.
- Letting Mend Pet and Revive Pet sit unused because they are not on the main bar.
- Spending mana on every ability every fight, so every third pull is spent sitting down drinking.
*Everything from here on is for players who have played WoW before: what Forever changed against the original game, and how well each claim is sourced.*
## Start with a correction
Several guides report that Auto Shot is no longer clipped by abilities. **That is a bug Blizzard is reverting.** From the community manager on 23 September: *"the 0.5-second cast time that precedes a Hunter's auto-shot or the use of a wand is not functioning properly... this is something that we will fix in an upcoming Beta build."* The 0.5-second cast is **intended**, and as of 30 September the fix had not shipped.
It is tempting to read the revert as a win for player feedback — the thread "Hunter is ruined" was edited to "WE ARE SAVED" when it was announced. **That was its author alone.** Blizzard's own announcement thread drew 136 replies overwhelmingly asking Blizzard *not* to fix it: *"Hunters currently feel so incredibly smooth to the point that the class should be balanced around this bug rather than reverting back to its clunky Classic version."*
## Misdirection: confirmed absent
Zero occurrences in the client-read spellbook diff — not among new, changed or unchanged spells. It is a Burning Crusade ability with no vanilla ancestor to diff against. One guide names it once in an introduction and never again in 800 lines; that is boilerplate, not evidence. **Hunters have no threat-transfer tool in Forever**, and neither does anyone else.
## Pets inherit your stats
The single most consequential change for level 60. The system is confirmed; the coefficients are player-derived, because the client does not expose them.
| Your stat | What the pet gets |
| --- | --- |
| Stamina | **2 pet health per point** |
| Armor | **30%** of yours |
| Attack Power | **10% of your highest** source |
| Critical Strike | **100% of yours** |
That crit line roughly doubles the value of crit for Beast Mastery.
**Other pet changes.** A **new Fox family** with *Trickster's Dance*. Owl renamed **Bird of Prey**. Eight families got new abilities — Bear **Swipe** hits three targets and is the AoE-threat pick. **Focus regeneration roughly doubled** to about 10 per second, continuous. **Furious Howl was rebuilt** into a one-minute party buff, then **nerfed 40%**. **Loyalty still exists, still gates training, and rises very slowly** — but once you learn an ability you can teach it to any future pet.
Two things to watch. **All pets swing at a base 2.0 seconds** with a passive restoring the classic speed, and **damage is not normalized to speed**, so a fast pet is simply better and nobody knows if that is intended. And **unlearning pet training points also unlearns the attack-speed passive**, permanently changing your pet's swing.
Do not trust the common claim that happiness decays much more slowly — part of what testers measured was **a bug where happiness stopped decreasing at max loyalty**, fixed on 24 September.
## Talents
| Tree | 11 | **16** | 21 | 31 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beast Mastery | Bestial Swiftness | **Summon Hawk** | Intimidation | Bestial Wrath |
| Marksmanship | **Lone Wolf** | Trueshot Aura | Scatter Shot | **Sniper Shot** |
| Survival | Deterrence | Counterattack | **Strider Kick** | **Lacerating Strikes** |
**Marksmanship got the most interesting new talent in the class. Lone Wolf, at 11 points: "You deal 20% increased damage with all attacks while you do not have an active pet."** That makes petless Marksmanship a real build for the first time in vanilla's lineage. *Careful Aim* gives **+20% of your Intellect as generic attack power**. **Aimed Shot is no longer a talent** — it is trained.
**Survival is the real rework.** *Improved Tracking* gives +1% damage per rank against **whatever creature type you are tracking**. **Entrapment was rewritten** from a 5% chance of a 5-second root into "when your traps are triggered, **all affected targets** are Entrapped" — a guaranteed root that is **on-demand with no diminishing returns**. *Survival Tactics* makes trap resistance a **hit** problem, which is the mechanical hook for Blizzard's stated trap-reliability rationale. And **Expose Prey** gives attacks against a marked target a chance to **activate Mongoose Bite**, finally unchaining it from being dodge-only. Removed: **Wyvern Sting**, Improved Feign Death, Humanoid Slaying, Killer Instinct.
## Abilities
**Traps are usable in combat.** The out-of-combat clause is deleted from all four tooltips, cooldowns went 15 to 30 seconds, and exclusivity is now **per school** — one Fire and one Frost trap should coexist, though a bug currently prevents it.
**Aimed Shot and Multi-Shot now share a 6-second cooldown**, which collapses the Marksmanship rotation into a single node. Aimed Shot's cast dropped to 2 seconds and its flat bonus damage was slashed hard — rank 6 from 600 to 166. **Multi-Shot lost ranks 2 through 5 entirely.**
**Serpent Sting now scales with ranged attack power, can crit, and is dynamic rather than snapshotted.** **Scorpid Sting was rewritten to reduce the target's hit chance by 2%.** **Volley lost its one-minute cooldown.** **Aspect of the Beast is now a damage aspect**, and **Aspect of the Monkey gained a 100-yard range so the pet benefits from it**.
**Disengage is still the vanilla melee-range threat dump, not the retail leap.** **The dead zone is still in the game** — 0 to 8 yards — and Blizzard has made **no statement** about it despite four heavy threads.
## Rotation
**At 20 all three specs are identical**, because nothing has diverged: Hunter's Mark, let the pet attack first, Serpent Sting, **Aimed Shot on cooldown**, Auto Shot filler, Disengage if you steal aggro, Arcane Shot only when mana-capped, Multi-Shot against several targets.
**At 60**, derived from tooltips and never played: **Beast Mastery** revolves around keeping two hawks up, so Arcane Shot becomes the filler you use when a hawk would be wasted. **Marksmanship** is the shared 6-second node plus maintained Serpent Sting — **Sniper Shot is situational, not rotational**, and one guide judges its cast time may make it a damage loss in PvE. **Survival** is genuinely melee: react to Mongoose Bite windows, Strider Kick on cooldown, traps woven with Resourcefulness paying the mana. **Dual wield is not mandatory** — Strider Kick scales with weapon damage, which testers say makes two-handers viable rather than stat sticks.
**A Survival landmine:** beta players report **melee attacks currently reset the ranged swing timer**, badly affecting weaving, and it is unclear whether that is intended. Traps also **do not scale with attack power**, which caps the trap-centric build.
## Stats
**At 20:** weapon damage far above everything, then Agility. Prefer **slower, higher-damage weapons** because Aimed Shot derives from weapon damage.
**At 60:** weapon damage, Agility, then **Hit** — which is worth more to a Forever Hunter than a Classic one, because one stat now buys melee hit, ranged hit, **trap reliability** and **Feign Death reliability**. Then crit, which **transfers 100% to your pet**.
**Separate ranged weapon skills survive.** Bows, Crossbows and Guns are still distinct, and at 300 skill the effective hit cap against bosses is **9%**, with 5 weapon skill worth roughly 3% hit. The *stat on items* was gutted while the *skill* stayed intact, so the practical route to the cap is now hit itself.
## Race
**The weapon-specialization crit racials do not work for ranged attacks, and this is the most important race fact in the class.** In-game stat-window screenshots settled what the guides disagreed about: Human Sword, Orc Axe and Dwarf Mace Specialization feed your **pet, your melee, Arcane Shot and Serpent Sting — but not Aimed Shot, Multi-Shot or Auto Shot.** The decisive comparison is that Elune's Light carries the *same* "all spells and attacks" wording and **does** apply to ranged.
A player put this to Blizzard directly and **got no reply**, so whether it is a bug or the design is unknown. As it stands, **the newly-added Human Hunter gets nothing from its own racial.**
**Tauren is called the single strongest choice across both factions** — Endurance now grants **+5% health and +1% hit**, and **Plainsrunning** lets you stutter-step-kite from level 1 while keeping Aspect of the Hawk up. **Night Elf is the Alliance pick**, on Elune's Light and Shadowmeld now working in combat. **Dwarf Hunter is a real grievance** across three threads — Gun Specialization is gone, replaced by a crit racial on a weapon class Hunters cannot use.
## Sentiment
Mostly aimed at preserved vanilla friction rather than the new design. Lone Wolf, melee Survival, hawks, in-combat traps and pet scaling are broadly welcomed. The anger is at the **dead zone**, **stable slots** (*"basically a deal breaker for me personally"*), **quivers and ammo pouches**, **Dwarf racials**, and the fact that **the tame-to-learn-pet-skills system is never explained in-game**.
One balance pass touched Hunter, on 24 September: Furious Howl −40%, a Tame Beast anti-exploit, two bug fixes, and a Strider Kick buff. That is all of it.
## Still unknown
Whether weapon-spec racial crit is *meant* to exclude ranged. Exact pet inheritance coefficients. Whether pet damage normalization to speed is intended. Whether the Auto Shot revert has shipped. And **Black Arrow**, which the client diff lists as an unchanged Hunter spell despite no vanilla equivalent existing — most likely a data artefact.
