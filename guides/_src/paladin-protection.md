## What Protection is for
**Holding four enemies at once, and taunting without spending a talent point.** [[Seal of Fury]] is learned at level 10 and judging it taunts, which Blizzard confirms is baseline rather than a talent. [[Consecration]] is baseline at 20 for every Paladin, and [[Righteous Fury]] raises the threat of the Holy damage both of them deal, which is why Spell Power is a tanking stat here.
**Four is the number to plan pulls around.** Blizzard redesigned [[Consecration]] so it deals moderate damage to the first 4 enemies to enter it and very low damage to everyone else. Its stated reason is parity: a Paladin should generate reliable threat on four targets at a time, the same as a Warrior's Thunderclap, because Blizzard wants Forever to keep Classic Era's combat feel instead of sliding into the AoE-focused play of later expansions. Pull four, grind them down, and expect nothing dependable from the ring past that. Mana is the other limit.
**Area grinding is not what it was.** Wowhead's levelling page, updated November 6, says the Paladin's ability to grind packs was "massively nerfed" by the [[Retribution Aura]] and [[Consecration]] changes, that few places between 20 and 30 reward it, and that 30 to 60 is still unknown. It still rates the spec a strong leveller because it handles several enemies at once and heals itself.
**Dungeons pay less than Classic.** Dungeon quests reward 50% less extra experience beyond normal quest values. Kills inside dungeons launched at 40% of their Classic Era experience and went to 60% on November 8. Icy Veins' case for levelling as Protection is that a tank finds groups easily, not that dungeons are fast.

One correction to that order is ours. As published it takes `Sacred Arbiter` at level 58 with 14 points in Retribution, and the talent needs 15. Take the third point of `Sanctified Judgement` first, as the list above does *(ours, not a source's)*.
Wowhead's Standard raid build, updated November 4, is the same 35 Protection talents with `Deflection` in place of `Benediction`. So the two sites agree on the tree and differ on one row-1 pick. Wowhead says `Anticipation` is the flexible talent: move points out of it as gear brings you to the Defense cap.
**Why these talents, briefly:**
- `Redoubt` is **a 10% chance when you are hit to raise your Block chance, up to 30% at full rank**, for 10 seconds or 5 blocks. Blizzard's October dive and ClassicWoW.gg print 20%. The client moved it back to 30% on October 30 and Icy Veins' current calculator agrees.
- `Precision` is +1% hit per rank on every spell and attack. Blizzard says the point is to make key abilities land, **the taunt from [[Seal of Fury]]'s Judgement included**.
- `Sacred Duty` takes up to a minute off the cooldowns of [[Divine Shield]], `Divine Protection` and `Templar's Bulwark`, and up to 10 seconds off Forbearance.
- `Shield Specialization` needs `Redoubt` maxed. Wowhead notes it returns more mana the more enemies hit you, and that `Sanctified Judgement` returns more on a single target.
- `Sacred Arbiter` is optional by Wowhead's account: your swings already refresh your own Judgements. It earns its point in a raid with a Holy Paladin and no Retribution Paladin.
**Other published 51-point builds**, all Wowhead's unless named, all dated November 4: a 23/28/0 "Shockprot" build that adds [[Holy Shock]] and `Consecrated Ground` for single-target threat and gives up `Shieldsworn` (Icy Veins publishes the same shape as Deep Holy and recommends it only when you are well geared); a 0/21/30 "Retprot" build for area damage; and a 0/32/19 dungeon build. Wowhead says the dungeon build's gain over Standard is too small to pay for a respec.
## Rotation
**Multi-target is the default here**, which is unusual for a tank.
1. **[[Righteous Fury]]** up at all times
2. **[[Seal of Fury]]** maintained: bonus Holy damage on every swing, an absorb worth 50% of that damage, and the taunt
3. **[[Judgement]]**: damage, and your taunt while Seal of Fury is up. Hold it when you expect to lose something
4. **[[Consecration]]** as often as mana allows, for threat on four
5. **`Holy Shield`** once you have it, at 34 or 42 depending on your order
6. **[[Holy Strike]]** on cooldown
7. **[[Exorcism]]** against Undead and Demons; keep a Blessing and an Aura up
8. **[[Hammer of Wrath]]** from 44 and **[[Holy Wrath]]** from 50 only when little or nothing is hitting you. Both have cast times. Wowhead limits Hammer of Wrath to tanking one target or none, and both sites limit Holy Wrath to when you are not actively tanking
**Why that order, and when it changes:**
- **[[Judgement]] takes no global cooldown,** so it never competes with the buttons below it. Wowhead and WoWSims' level 60 list both press it first *(WoWSims' model)*. Icy Veins lists [[Consecration]] above it. That list fights one boss and has no add to pick up, so holding it for a loose enemy is our call *(ours, not a source's)*.
- **[[Judgement]] is a ranged attack since November 11.** Blizzard changed it to use your ranged hit chance and to stop counting as a melee attack for item and effect procs. A Judgement that misses is still a taunt that failed, so Hit keeps its value. The published stat pages predate the change and still file Judgement under melee.
- **On one enemy, [[Consecration]] is a poor trade.** Rank 1 costs 135 Mana for 48 damage on one target before Spell Power, and 192 across four. [[Holy Strike]] costs 12 at level 20. WoWSims keeps the ring above [[Holy Strike]] on a single boss, but that is at 60 in raid gear.
- **Sync your buttons when mana is short.** Wowhead's tip: press [[Judgement]] and [[Holy Strike]] together and time Seal refreshes and [[Consecration]] into the same moment, so the gaps between casts are long enough for mana regeneration to restart.
- **Lay the ring as they arrive.** It ticks once a second for 8 seconds, so a tick on empty ground is mana gone. Its cooldown is also 8 seconds, so one cast can follow the last with no overlap.
- **Recast [[Seal of Fury]] when it has gone, not before.** It lasts 30 seconds, [[Judgement]] leaves it standing and a recast adds no second shield. WoWSims casts it only once it is down *(WoWSims' model)*.
- **`Holy Shield` wants four blocks.** WoWSims puts it above [[Holy Strike]] at 60 *(WoWSims' model)*. Rank 1 costs 100 Mana and deals 300 if all 4 charges go inside its 10 seconds, where rank 2 of [[Consecration]] deals 320 across four enemies for 235. It costs you the same when you block only once. Ranks 2 to 4 train at 40, 50 and 60.
- **With `Shieldsworn`, cast `Holy Shield` before [[Holy Strike]].** The capstone makes each [[Holy Strike]] set off `Holy Shield` and `Shield Specialization` without spending their charges or cooldowns, and lets you block spells for 6 seconds. It also brings [[Holy Strike]] to a 6-second cooldown, which Wowhead points out keeps `Iron Creed`'s 6-second damage reduction up permanently.
- **Skip the Crusader opener on a pack.** WoWSims opens a boss by judging [[Seal of the Crusader]] first, and Wowhead judges an assigned Seal first on anything that will live 20 to 30 seconds. That debuff sits on one enemy and puts your only [[Judgement]] on its 10-second cooldown, so the taunt is gone when the pull goes wrong *(ours, not a source's)*.
**Judging no longer costs you the Seal.** Blizzard states [[Judgement]] does not consume the Paladin's active Seal, so taunting with it leaves [[Seal of Fury]] running and the next taunt comes back with the cooldown. The taunt also reaches further than any other tank's: Blizzard puts it at 10 yards, because it is delivered by Judgement, where Druid Growl and Warrior Taunt reach 5. Use that reach to pick up an add before it arrives.
**The absorb does not stack.** Re-applying [[Seal of Fury]] refreshes the shield rather than adding a second one, per Blizzard, so there is nothing to gain from stacking applications. A Priest's `Power Word: Shield` does not overwrite it, per Blizzard's notes, so you can carry both.
**Single target:** same order, but drop [[Consecration]] to conserve mana.
**Swapping seals removes your taunt** *(ours, not a source's)*. `Swift Judgement` gives you a second one: a Judgement on its own 1-minute cooldown at 10 yards, in row 4 behind `Templar's Bulwark`. It does not consume your Seal and does not share a cooldown with [[Judgement]], so with [[Seal of Fury]] up it taunts when your main Judgement cannot *(ours, not a source's)*.
**[[Divine Shield]] does not have to drop the boss.** Wowhead describes judging [[Seal of Fury]] from inside the bubble to force an enemy onto you for 3 seconds while you are immune, and 6 seconds with `Swift Judgement` added. The bubble still halves your damage.
## Stat priority
**The sources no longer put Spell Power first for a main tank.** Icy Veins' level-60 list is Stamina, Defense to 440, Spell Power, Intellect, Strength. Wowhead publishes three lists and tells you to start from the balanced one: Stamina, Spell Power, Hit to 8%, Defense to 440, Armor. Its offensive list leads with Spell Power and its defensive list pushes it below Block. Both give 440 Defense as the point where a boss can no longer critically hit you.
**While levelling, weapon first.** Icy Veins says to take Weapon Damage, Hit and critical strike early because your swings are most of your damage, and that Spell Power starts to matter once you have [[Retribution Aura]] and [[Consecration]]. Its levelling list then runs Stamina, Spell Power, Strength, Block, Hit, Defense, Armor.
**Why Spell Power is on the list at all.** A Paladin's threat *is* Holy damage: [[Seal of Fury]], [[Consecration]] and [[Holy Strike]], all amplified by [[Righteous Fury]]. Blizzard's notes give [[Consecration]] 0.042 of your Spell Power per tick, about a third of it across the 8 ticks of a full cast *(our arithmetic)*. `Precision` covers spells as well as swings.
**[[Righteous Fury]] raises all your threat by 33%, and the threat of your Holy damage by another 90%**, per Blizzard's November 2 notes. You train it at level 10, the same level other tanks get their tanking stance, and the Legacy perk `The Quick and the Dead` makes it free too.
**Nobody's threat is cut for you any more.** Blizzard removed `Salvation`, so the damage dealers in your group keep all their threat, and the 33% on everything is what Righteous Fury gives you to stay ahead of them *(ours, not a source's)*. A Retribution Paladin with a two-hander is the exception: `Two-Handed Weapon Specialization` cuts their threat by up to 20%.
**Carry a shield and prefer a fast one-hander.** The client's [[Seal of Fury]] text makes the absorb conditional on having a shield equipped. Blizzard states the seal's bonus Holy damage is a constant amount per swing, unlike [[Seal of Righteousness]], and says outright that this favours a fast weapon while tanking. Swings per second scale both your threat and your absorb. Take mail over leather where the stats are close.
**Spell power weapons come later.** Wowhead flags a trap at low levels: a weapon with a heavy spell power roll carries roughly a 25% melee damage penalty, and the spell power scaling is too low early on to pay for it. It says a spell power weapon becomes the better pick "slightly higher in the levels", sooner if you fight several enemies at a time, and gives no level.

## What Forever changed for Protection
- **Six new talents:** `Sacred Duty` in row 2, `Templar's Bulwark` at the 11-point gold medal, `Swift Judgement` at the 16-point one, `Stoicism` in row 5 (up to 20% shorter Stun and Charm effects), `Iron Creed` in row 6, and the `Shieldsworn` capstone at 31 points, behind `Holy Shield`. `Shieldsworn` takes 4 seconds off [[Holy Strike]]'s cooldown, makes it strike with your shield, setting off `Holy Shield` and `Shield Specialization` without using their charges or cooldowns, and lets you block spells for 6 seconds.
- **`Templar's Bulwark` is the survival cooldown this spec never had.** Blizzard says Protection needed one like other tanks carry, and tied it into the Forbearance system on purpose. It is also the prerequisite for `Swift Judgement`.
- **`Iron Creed` makes [[Holy Strike]] a tanking button.** It gives up to 30% more threat from Holy Strike and 6% reduced damage taken for 6 seconds while [[Righteous Fury]] is active, at 3 points.
- **`Improved Righteous Fury` is a different talent under the same name**. It grants up to 9% reduced damage taken while Righteous Fury is active, 3 points in row 4, where Classic gave threat.
- `Precision` covers every spell and attack, not just melee weapon attacks. `Anticipation` sits in row 4 and reaches +20 Defense at max rank. `Toughness` sits in row 3, and `Divine Intellect` joins row 1 from the Holy tree. `One-Handed Weapon Specialization` and `Improved Seal of Fury` are gone, so [[Seal of Fury]] returns no mana.
- `Reckoning` now **also procs on a block**, 8% per rank to a 40% chance after blocking a melee attack, alongside 20% per rank to a 100% chance from a damaging critical hit against you. Blizzard added the block trigger because a tank is often immune to crits, and it can fire from blocks only once every 1.5 seconds so it does not reward tanking huge packs. You can store up to 4 extra attacks, and it performs one attack per stored stack. It no longer gains stacks while you sit, so sitting to drink does not charge it.
- **`Blessing of Kings` left the tree to become baseline at 20**, where the client has it. [[Consecration]] and [[Lasting Judgement]] are baseline now as well. `Blessing of Sanctuary`, `Improved Concentration Aura` and `Improved Devotion Aura` are gone from the class.
- **There is no group threat tool.** Blizzard removed `Salvation`, `Mass Salvation` and `Revoke`, so a Paladin has no threat-reduction spell to learn.
- [[Lay on Hands]] has a 20-minute cooldown, which puts a full heal inside a levelling session rather than once an hour.
- **[[Judgement]] became a ranged attack on November 11.** It uses ranged hit chance and no longer sets off melee item or effect procs.
## At level 60
**The published raid build is the 0/35/16 above**, on both sites. Neither has seen a raid: Barrow Deeps, Hyjal Summit and Onyxia's Lair open December 9, 2026. Wowhead says outright that if raid damage is a step up from Classic it will revisit its talent choices, and Icy Veins says it cannot yet value avoidance.
**One open question is specific: whether a 3-second conditional taunt can hold a raid boss.** `Swift Judgement` is the published answer, as a second taunt on a 1-minute cooldown and a backup when the first one misses. Icy Veins raises the opposite problem: with [[Seal of Fury]] up every [[Judgement]] taunts, so an off-tank swaps to an assigned Seal such as `Seal of Light` or `Seal of Wisdom` until it is their turn.
Blizzard's closing verdict is that Paladins can be highly effective tanks, with `Swift Judgement` and `Templar's Bulwark` as strong cooldowns tied to the Seal, Judgement and Forbearance systems, and baseline [[Consecration]] plus `Iron Creed` giving more consistent control of the battlefield. Blizzard is equally plain about the gaps: no interrupt, no way to slow enemy movement, and limited range that Holy talents extend but never to a caster's reach. The four-target ceiling is design, not tuning, so do not expect it to lift.
