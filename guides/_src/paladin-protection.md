## What Protection is for
**Holding four enemies at once, and taunting without spending a talent point.** [[Seal of Fury]] is learned at level 10 and judging it taunts, which Blizzard confirms is baseline rather than a talent. [[Consecration]] is baseline at 20 for every Paladin, and [[Righteous Fury]] raises the threat of the Holy damage both of them deal, which is why Spell Power leads your stat priority.
**Four is the number to plan pulls around.** Blizzard redesigned [[Consecration]] so it deals moderate damage to the first 4 enemies to enter it and very low damage to everyone else. Its stated reason is parity: a Paladin should generate reliable threat on four targets at a time, the same as a Warrior's Thunderclap, because Blizzard wants Forever to keep Classic Era's combat feel instead of sliding into the AoE-focused play of later expansions. Pull four, grind them down, and expect nothing dependable from the ring past that. Mana is the other limit.
**Dungeon levelling is a mixed picture for this spec.** It rested on [[Consecration]], its one source of damage to a whole pull, and one published guide called Forever's dungeon experience out as hurting this spec more than any other. Dungeon quests reward 50% less extra experience beyond normal quest values, a deliberate reduction in levelling speed, and kills inside dungeons pay about 20% more.
No source publishes a levelling score for any Paladin spec. Those experience figures and the guide's warning are what the sources say on levelling here.
## Your talent points
**Level 20: 0/11/0.** Our own extension, not a published build. The two published builds agreed on `Redoubt` and `Precision`, then spent on talents that have moved or left the tree, so we adjusted the rest.
1. `Redoubt`, 5 points. **A 10% chance when you are hit to raise your Block chance, up to 30% at full rank**, for 10 seconds or 5 blocks. Any damaging melee attack triggers it. `Divine Intellect` shares row 1, so there is an alternative first pick, but Block chance feeds `Holy Shield` and `Reckoning`'s block trigger, and Intellect does not *(ours, not a source's)*.
2. `Precision`, 3 points — +1% hit per rank in the client, so +3% here. Blizzard widened this talent to cover all your spells and attacks rather than melee weapon swings alone, and says the point is to make key abilities land, **the taunt from [[Seal of Fury]]'s Judgement included**. These three points buy taunt reliability.
3. `Sacred Duty`, 2 points. Up to a minute off the cooldowns of [[Divine Shield]], `Divine Protection` and `Templar's Bulwark`, and up to 10 seconds off Forbearance
4. `Templar's Bulwark`, 1 point, the 11-point gold medal and the survival cooldown you are here for
Named alternatives: `Divine Intellect` in place of `Sacred Duty`, because points in row 1 count toward the gates too, or `Shield Specialization` for the eleventh point. One rank gives 15% more absorbed and a 50% chance for a block to restore 4% of your maximum mana. `Improved Righteous Fury` and `Anticipation` sit in row 4, out of reach at 20.
**Level 30: 0/21/0.** Our own extension, not a published build: `Redoubt` 5, `Precision` 3, `Sacred Duty` 2, `Templar's Bulwark` 1, `Shield Specialization` 2, `Toughness` 2, `Swift Judgement` 1, `Improved Righteous Fury` 3, `Anticipation` 1, `Holy Shield` 1. That order spends 20 points before the last one, which is what the 21-point gold medal needs. `Reckoning` is the other candidate for that final point; `Holy Shield` wins it here because a tank wants the block and it leads to the `Shieldsworn` capstone *(ours, not a source's)*. At level 30 you get its first rank, 75 Holy damage per block for 100 Mana. `Iron Creed` is row 6, so it waits for the 26th point at level 35.
## Rotation
**Multi-target is the default here**, which is unusual for a tank.
1. **[[Righteous Fury]]** up at all times
2. **[[Seal of Fury]]** maintained: bonus Holy damage on every swing, an absorb worth 50% of that damage, and the taunt
3. **[[Consecration]]** as often as mana allows, for threat on four
4. **[[Judgement]]**: damage, and your taunt while Seal of Fury is up. Hold it when you expect to lose something
5. **[[Holy Strike]]** on cooldown
6. **[[Exorcism]]** against Undead and Demons; keep a Blessing and an Aura up
**Judging no longer costs you the Seal.** Blizzard states [[Judgement]] does not consume the Paladin's active Seal, so taunting with it leaves [[Seal of Fury]] running and the next taunt comes back with the cooldown. The taunt also reaches further than any other tank's: Blizzard puts it at 10 yards, because it is delivered by Judgement, where Druid Growl and Warrior Taunt reach 5. Use that reach to pick up an add before it arrives.
**The absorb does not stack.** Re-applying [[Seal of Fury]] refreshes the shield rather than adding a second one, per Blizzard, so there is nothing to gain from stacking applications.
**Single target:** same order, but drop [[Consecration]] to conserve mana.
**Swapping seals removes your taunt** *(ours, not a source's)*. `Swift Judgement` gives you a second one: a Judgement on its own 1-minute cooldown at 10 yards, in row 4 behind `Templar's Bulwark`. It does not consume your Seal and does not share a cooldown with [[Judgement]], so with [[Seal of Fury]] up it taunts when your main Judgement cannot *(ours, not a source's)*.
## Stat priority
**Spell Power**, then Hit, Stamina, Defense, Armor, Block, Intellect, Strength, Agility.
Spell Power first is correct here *(ours, not a source's)*. In Forever a Paladin's threat *is* Holy damage: [[Seal of Fury]], [[Consecration]] and [[Holy Strike]], all amplified by [[Righteous Fury]]. Hit earns its second place twice over, since `Precision` now covers spells and a resisted Judgement is a failed taunt.
**[[Righteous Fury]] raises all your threat by 33%, and the threat of your Holy attacks by another 90%.** Its tooltip shows only the 90%. Most of your threat still comes from the volume of Holy damage you put out yourself, which is the case for Spell Power restated.
**Carry a shield and prefer a fast one-hander.** The client's [[Seal of Fury]] text makes the absorb conditional on having a shield equipped. Blizzard states the seal's bonus Holy damage is a constant amount per swing, unlike [[Seal of Righteousness]], and says outright that this favours a fast weapon while tanking. Swings per second scale both your threat and your absorb. Take mail over leather where the stats are close.
**Leave spell power weapons alone below 20.** Wowhead flags a trap here: a weapon with a heavy spell power roll carries roughly a 25% melee damage penalty, and you do not have the spell-power talents yet to make that trade pay off. A plain melee weapon out-damages it until you hit 20.

## What Forever changed for Protection
- **Six new talents:** `Sacred Duty` in row 2, `Templar's Bulwark` at the 11-point gold medal, `Swift Judgement` at the 16-point one, `Stoicism` in row 5 (up to 20% shorter Stun and Charm effects), `Iron Creed` in row 6, and the `Shieldsworn` capstone at 31 points, behind `Holy Shield`. `Shieldsworn` takes 4 seconds off [[Holy Strike]]'s cooldown, makes it strike with your shield, setting off `Holy Shield` and `Shield Specialization` without using their charges or cooldowns, and lets you block spells for 6 seconds.
- **`Templar's Bulwark` is the survival cooldown this spec never had.** Blizzard says Protection needed one like other tanks carry, and tied it into the Forbearance system on purpose. It is also the prerequisite for `Swift Judgement`.
- **`Iron Creed` makes [[Holy Strike]] a tanking button.** It gives up to 30% more threat from Holy Strike and 6% reduced damage taken for 6 seconds while [[Righteous Fury]] is active, at 3 points.
- **`Improved Righteous Fury` is a different talent under the same name**. It grants up to 9% reduced damage taken while Righteous Fury is active, 3 points in row 4, where Classic gave threat.
- `Precision` covers every spell and attack, not just melee weapon attacks. `Anticipation` sits in row 4 and reaches +20 Defense at max rank. `Toughness` sits in row 3, and `Divine Intellect` joins row 1 from the Holy tree. `One-Handed Weapon Specialization` and `Improved Seal of Fury` are gone, so [[Seal of Fury]] returns no mana.
- `Reckoning` now **also procs on a block**, 8% per rank to a 40% chance after blocking a melee attack, alongside 20% per rank to a 100% chance from a damaging critical hit against you. Blizzard added the block trigger because a tank is often immune to crits, and it can fire from blocks only once every 1.5 seconds so it does not reward tanking huge packs. You can store up to 4 extra attacks.
- `Holy Shield` is the 21-point gold medal in row 5: a 30% chance to Block for 10 seconds over 4 charges, dealing 75 Holy damage per block at level 30, 110 at 40 and 153 at 50, and that damage carries 20% additional threat.
- **`Blessing of Kings` left the tree to become baseline at 20**, where the client has it. [[Consecration]] and [[Lasting Judgement]] are baseline now as well. `Blessing of Sanctuary`, `Improved Concentration Aura` and `Improved Devotion Aura` are gone from the class.
- **There is no group threat tool.** Trainers teach no `Blessing of Salvation`, `Salvation`, `Mass Salvation` or `Revoke`, so a Paladin has no threat-reduction spell to learn.
- [[Lay on Hands]] has a 20-minute cooldown, which puts a full heal inside a levelling session rather than once an hour.
## At level 60
Unknown, and one open question is specific: **whether a 3-second conditional taunt can hold a raid boss.** Blizzard's closing verdict is that Paladins can be highly effective tanks, with `Swift Judgement` and `Templar's Bulwark` as strong cooldowns tied to the Seal, Judgement and Forbearance systems, and baseline [[Consecration]] plus `Iron Creed` giving more consistent control of the battlefield. Blizzard is equally plain about the gaps: no interrupt, no way to slow enemy movement, and limited range that Holy talents extend but never to a caster's reach. The four-target ceiling is design, not tuning, so do not expect it to lift.
