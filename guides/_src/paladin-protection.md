## What Protection is for
**Holding four enemies at once, and taunting without spending a talent point.** [[Seal of Fury]] is learned at level 10 and judging it taunts, which Blizzard confirms is baseline rather than a talent. [[Consecration]] is baseline at 20 for every Paladin, and [[Righteous Fury]] amplifies the Holy damage both of them deal, which is why Spell Power leads your stat priority.
**Four is the number to plan pulls around.** Blizzard redesigned [[Consecration]] so it deals moderate damage to the first 4 enemies to enter it and very low damage to everyone else. Its stated reason is parity: a Paladin should generate reliable threat on four targets at a time, the same as a Warrior's Thunderclap, because Blizzard wants Forever to keep Classic Era's combat feel instead of sliding into the AoE-focused play of later expansions. Pull four, grind them down, and expect nothing dependable from the ring past that. Mana is the other limit.
**Forever nerfed the thing this spec used to be good at.** Protection Paladin's dungeon levelling rested on [[Consecration]], its one source of damage to a whole pull. Forever cut dungeon experience hard, and one published guide calls that out as hurting this spec more than any other. A later beta build cut the extra experience on dungeon quests by 50%, a deliberate reduction in levelling speed.
No source publishes a levelling score for any Paladin spec. That experience cut and the guide's warning about it are what the sources say on levelling here.
## Your talent points
**Level 20: 0/11/0.** Two published builds agree on the first ten points.
1. `Redoubt`, 5 points. **A 10% chance when you are hit to raise your Block chance, up to 20% at full rank**, for 10 seconds or 5 blocks. Plan around that 20%, though the client at 1.60.1.70374 still reads 30%. Blizzard also removed the old requirement to be hit by a critical strike, so it triggers off any hit. `Toughness` now shares row 1 and the client gives it up to 10% more Armor from items, so there is an alternative first pick, but Block chance is what feeds `Holy Shield` and `Reckoning`'s block trigger, and armour from items is not *(ours, not a source's)*.
2. `Precision`, 3 points — +1% hit per rank in the client, so +3% here. Blizzard widened this talent to cover all your spells and attacks rather than melee weapon swings alone, and says the point is to make key abilities land, **the taunt from [[Seal of Fury]]'s Judgement included**. These three points buy taunt reliability.
3. `Anticipation`, 2 points
4. The eleventh point is either **`Shield Specialization`** or `Improved Seal of Fury`
Blizzard calls `Improved Seal of Fury` the 11-point gold medal talent, so it is that eleventh point exactly, and it costs one. Named alternatives: `Improved Righteous Fury`, which grants up to 6% reduced damage taken while [[Righteous Fury]] is active, or `Sacred Duty`, which Blizzard gives as up to 4% total Stamina plus up to a minute off the cooldowns of [[Divine Shield]], `Divine Protection` and `Templar's Bulwark`. If you do not want the hit, max `Anticipation` instead of `Precision`.
**Wowhead currently recommends this Retribution dip outright**, not as a fallback: `Holy Conduit` and `Benediction` give more mana retention right now than `Improved Seal of Fury` does. Part of that gap is a live bug. [[Seal of Fury]] is granting its `Improved Seal of Fury` mana return even on builds that never talented it. Wowhead expects the two routes to even out once that is fixed, and once more `Shield Specialization` ranks unlock at 30. The dip trades durability for uptime, and no exact point total is published for it.
**Level 30: 0/21/0.** Our own extension, not a published build: `Redoubt` 5, `Precision` 3, `Anticipation` 4, `Improved Seal of Fury` 1, `Improved Righteous Fury` 3, `Swift Judgement` 1, `One-Handed Weapon Specialization` 3, `Templar's Bulwark` 1. That order spends 20 points before the last one, which is what the 21-point gold medal needs. `Reckoning` is the other candidate for that final point; `Templar's Bulwark` wins it here because a 100%-health absorb beats extra attacks for a tank, and because `Holy Shield` sits behind it *(ours, not a source's)*. `Iron Creed` is row 6, 25 points deep, so the beta's cap cannot reach it at all.
## Rotation
**Multi-target is the default here**, which is unusual for a tank.
1. **[[Righteous Fury]]** up at all times
2. **[[Seal of Fury]]** maintained: bonus Holy damage on every swing, an absorb worth 50% of that damage, and the taunt
3. **[[Consecration]]** as often as mana allows, for threat on four
4. **[[Judgement]]**: damage, and your taunt while Seal of Fury is up. Hold it when you expect to lose something
5. **[[Holy Strike]]** on cooldown
6. **[[Exorcism]]** against Undead and Demons; keep a Blessing and an Aura up
**Judging no longer costs you the Seal.** Blizzard states [[Judgement]] does not consume the Paladin's active Seal, so taunting with it leaves [[Seal of Fury]] running and the next taunt comes back with the cooldown. The taunt also reaches further than any other tank's: Blizzard puts it at 10 yards, because it is delivered by Judgement, where Druid Growl and Warrior Taunt reach 5. Use that reach to pick up an add before it arrives.
**The absorb does not stack.** Re-applying [[Seal of Fury]] refreshes the shield rather than adding a second one, per Blizzard, so there is nothing to gain from stacking applications.
**Single target:** same order, but drop [[Consecration]] to conserve mana.
**Swapping seals removes your taunt** *(ours, not a source's)*. `Swift Judgement` is the answer: no mana cost, a 1-minute cooldown, it finishes [[Judgement]]'s remaining cooldown and makes the next one free. Blizzard's stated intent is reacting to a resisted taunt, or taunting several enemies in quick succession. Do not press it for damage.
## Stat priority
**Spell Power**, then Hit, Stamina, Defense, Armor, Block, Intellect, Strength, Agility.
Spell Power first genuinely is correct here. In Forever a Paladin's threat *is* Holy damage: [[Seal of Fury]], [[Consecration]] and [[Holy Strike]], all amplified by [[Righteous Fury]]. Hit earns its second place twice over, since `Precision` now covers spells and a resisted Judgement is a failed taunt.
**[[Righteous Fury]]'s threat bonus is deliberately smaller than Classic's**, and Blizzard says why: Protection Paladins have access to more Holy damage than they did in Classic Era, so the multiplier does not need to be as high. The live figure is a 60% increase to Holy threat. Read that as a steer rather than a nerf. More of your threat now comes from the volume of Holy damage you put out yourself, which is the case for Spell Power restated.
**Carry a shield and prefer a fast one-hander.** The client's [[Seal of Fury]] text makes the absorb conditional on having a shield equipped. Blizzard states the seal's bonus Holy damage is a constant amount per swing, unlike [[Seal of Righteousness]], and says outright that this favours a fast weapon while tanking. Swings per second scale both your threat and your absorb. Take mail over leather where the stats are close.
**Leave spell power weapons alone below 20.** Wowhead flags a trap here: a weapon with a heavy spell power roll carries roughly a 25% melee damage penalty, and you do not have the spell-power talents yet to make that trade pay off. A plain melee weapon out-damages it until you hit 20.

## What Forever changed for Protection
- **Five new talents:** `Improved Seal of Fury`, `Sacred Duty`, `Swift Judgement`, `Templar's Bulwark`, and `Iron Creed`.
- **`Templar's Bulwark` is the survival cooldown this spec never had.** Blizzard says Protection needed one like other tanks carry, and tied it into the Forbearance system on purpose. `Holy Shield` now sits behind it as a prerequisite.
- **`Iron Creed` makes [[Holy Strike]] a tanking button.** Blizzard gives it as **up to 15% more threat from Holy Strike, and up to 10% reduced damage taken for 6 seconds** while [[Righteous Fury]] is active, and those are the figures to plan around — though the client at 1.60.1.70374 reads 30% and 15%, double both.
- **`Improved Righteous Fury` is a different talent under the same name**. It grants up to 6% reduced damage taken while Righteous Fury is active, where Classic gave threat.
- `Precision` covers every spell and attack, not just melee weapon attacks. `Anticipation` moved up a row and reaches +20 Defense at max rank, which is what the client reads too. `Toughness` moved up to row 1. `One-Handed Weapon Specialization` moved to row 4 and needs only 3 points for its full effect, at +2% one-handed damage per rank in the client.
- `Reckoning` now **also procs on a block**, up to a 40% chance after blocking a melee attack, alongside its up to 100% chance from a damaging critical hit against you. Blizzard added the block trigger because a tank is often immune to crits, and capped the whole talent at one proc every 1.5 seconds so it does not reward tanking huge packs. The client confirms the block trigger is in the game, but its whole description is one line — extra attacks after blocking — with no per-rank figure, so this page carries none.
- **Blizzard has published two `Reckoning` bugs and the game still has them.** It does not let you save up 4 charges, and its extra attacks can interrupt your current auto-attack swing timer. Blizzard says it plans to fix both in an upcoming patch, so do not judge the talent on how it behaves today.
- `Holy Shield` deals 110 Holy damage per block and grants a 30% chance to Block for 10 seconds, over 4 charges, and that damage carries 20% additional threat. Blizzard now gates it behind `Templar's Bulwark`.
- **`Blessing of Kings` left the tree to become baseline at 20**, where the client has it. [[Consecration]] and [[Lasting Judgement]] are baseline now as well. `Blessing of Sanctuary` and `Improved Concentration Aura` are gone from the class, which Blizzard confirms and the client seconds for Sanctuary: it has no row at all. This page also recorded `Improved Devotion Aura` as removed, which the dive does not address either way.
- **A group threat tool is published and not in the game.** `Salvation` is no longer a Blessing, so a Paladin can keep both a Blessing and `Salvation` on the same ally, and the raid version that targets a whole class is now called `Mass Salvation`. Blizzard's own note on it: this change is not yet live and is going into a future beta build. The client says the same thing from the other side — it still has [[Blessing of Salvation]], a Blessing learned at 26 that cuts all threat by 30%, and no row for either `Salvation` or `Mass Salvation`.
- [[Lay on Hands]] has a 20-minute cooldown, which puts a full heal inside a levelling session rather than once an hour.
## At level 60
Unknown, and one open question is specific: **whether a 4-second conditional taunt can hold a raid boss.** Blizzard's closing verdict is that Paladins can be highly effective tanks, with `Swift Judgement` and `Templar's Bulwark` as strong cooldowns tied to the Seal, Judgement and Forbearance systems, and baseline [[Consecration]] plus `Iron Creed` giving more consistent control of the battlefield. Blizzard is equally plain about the gaps: no interrupt, no way to slow enemy movement, and limited range that Holy talents extend but never to a caster's reach. The four-target ceiling is design, not tuning, so do not expect it to lift.
The [[Consecration]] bug Wowhead reported is fixed: it left targets inside the ring not taking its full damage, worst near the edge. An earlier beta build repaired Consecration's failure to apply its increased damage effect to some targets, its inconsistent Z axis, and a case where it pulled targets vertically above or below the caster.
