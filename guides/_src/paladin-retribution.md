Two-handed melee with Holy magic layered on top. **[[Holy Strike]] at level 6** turns it from auto-attack-and-wait into something you actually play, twenty levels earlier than Classic gave a Paladin a button.

Levelling guide, 1 to 60. The beta is capped at **level 30**, so no level-60 Retribution build has been tested. Blizzard published its Paladin class deep dive on October 15, the last of the series, and this page is written from it and then read back against the client at build 1.60.1.70374. Where Blizzard speaks, Blizzard is the source; where it says nothing, the client is, and figures that are only a guide site's are marked.
Talents open at 10. Blizzard keeps the one-point gold medal talents at 11, 21 and 31 points and has added a new one at 16 points, which for Retribution is `Sacred Arbiter` at around level 25. `Repentance` sits at 21 points now and `Twist of Light` at 31. Dual Specialization opens at 40. Blizzard's own caveat: "class abilities and talents may continue to evolve throughout the beta test and are subject to additional changes."
**`Crusade` is almost certainly still spendable.** A widely-published level-30 build puts two points in it, and Blizzard's removal list for this tree names only `Improved Retribution Aura` and `Sanctity Aura`. The client carries `Crusade` with working text: 2% to all damage, and 2% more against Demons and Undead. Neither of those proves the tree can reach it, because the client keeps a record after a talent leaves a tree, so confirm it in game before you commit the points. `Improved Holy Strike` is in exactly the same position.
**`Champion of the Light` converts up to 30% of your Intellect into spell damage.** That is Blizzard's own talent line, so that is the figure this page publishes, while the same article's designer note describes a cut to 60% and the client still reads 100% — the magnitude is still moving, so build for the direction rather than the number.
Three kinds of caveat run through these pages and they do not mean the same thing. Something marked as ours is our own reasoning rather than a source's. A client or a guide-site reading is a number Blizzard has not published. The third case is Blizzard having published something the game does not have yet: on this page that is `Improved Seal of the Crusader`, where Blizzard says the Judgement bonus is baseline now and the client's talent still grants it.
**The hover tooltips on this page come from the client index at build 1.60.1.70374 — the same build this page was checked against — not from Blizzard's article.** Where a tooltip and the prose disagree, believe the prose: it is Blizzard's figure, and the tooltip is the number the game is showing.
Wowhead reports that beta testers and class discords consider Retribution the single most overtuned spec in the level-20 bracket, and expects changes. Treat the talent and gearing advice below as a snapshot.

## What Retribution is for
**Layering Holy damage onto a two-handed swing, with a real button to press from level 6.** [[Seal of Command]] at 20 procs Holy damage scaled off the swing, and a stun amplifies its Judgement.
Blizzard's framing for the spec is three things: new ways to sustain Judgements, weapon attacks blended with Holy damage, and a more approachable form of Seal Twisting. Sustaining Judgements starts in row 3 with `Sanctified Judgement`. The Seal Twisting arrives only at 31 points, far past the beta's cap.
Wowhead's overtuning warning is the only published comment aimed squarely at this spec. It is not a levelling score: no source publishes one for any Paladin spec, and a score would only measure solo kill speed.
**You have no area damage until [[Consecration]] at 20.** Blizzard trains it to every Paladin now instead of selling it as a Holy talent, and it only damages four enemies properly. Pull one target at a time.
**Blizzard states the class has no interrupt and no way to slow an enemy's movement**, and that Holy talents widen its range without matching other mana users. Your own speed comes from one talent, `Pursuit of Justice`. The published guides still call the class *"slow, very slow."*
**Treat mana as an extension of your health bar.** Topping up with [[Holy Light]] and killing the next thing at low mana usually beats sitting down to drink.
## Your talent points
**Level 20: 0/0/11.**
1. `Deflection`, 5 points. The client's Paladin row reads **1% parry** a rank, so five points is +5%, and Blizzard has it in row 1, so it is available from level 10
2. `Improved Judgement`, 2 points. The client takes **1 second** off [[Judgement]]'s 10-second cooldown
3. `Conviction`, **3 points**, and that is forced: [[Seal of Command]] is a tier-3 talent needing 10 points in the tree, and 5+2+1 would not reach it. Blizzard moved `Conviction` up to row 2 and reworded it to say plainly that the critical strike chance applies to melee attacks; the client reads **5% melee critical strike chance** and publishes no rank ladder, so what three points buy rather than the full talent is not stated anywhere
4. **[[Seal of Command]]** at 20
**The published respec at 20**, for mana rather than damage: `Benediction` 5, `Holy Conduit` 2, `Improved Judgement` 2, `Conviction` 1, [[Seal of Command]] 1. `Benediction` is broader and shallower than the version those builds were written against: Blizzard applies it to all instant-cast spells and abilities rather than to Seals and [[Judgement]] alone, and the client reads a flat **10%** where the old talent took up to 15% off those two.
**That makes the five-point `Benediction` dip the weaker half of the respec** *(ours, not a source's)*. Almost everything a Retribution Paladin presses at 20 is instant, so the discount does touch the whole rotation — but not [[Holy Light]], the mana sink this page tells you to treat as your health bar, and the buttons it gained are discounted far more deeply by `Holy Conduit` anyway. Take the 2 points of `Holy Conduit`, which makes [[Consecration]] affordable and cheapens [[Exorcism]] in Ruins of Lordaeron and Shadowfang Keep, and put `Benediction`'s five into damage instead.
A second published build drops `Improved Judgement` for two more `Conviction`, on the grounds that *"mana is simply not an issue at level 20 with the new changes."* That is the better-supported position.
**Wowhead goes further and questions the whole premise.** Its testers find [[Seal of Command]] *not meaningfully better than* [[Seal of Righteousness]] at 20, once you account for talents and spell power. Its own build spends that final point on `Divine Intellect`, `Voice of Truth` or mana regen instead, then aims at [[Holy Shock]] and `Champion of the Light` later.
Wowhead calls that Holy-leaning "Shockadin" build the stronger PvE choice right now. It rates this page's "Seal Twist" build weaker in PvE at 20, but ahead in PvP burst with a slow two-hander. Blizzard has since put [[Holy Shock]] at the Holy tree's 21-point gold medal, ten points earlier in that tree, which makes the hybrid a cheaper plan than it was.
**Neither build can actually twist seals.** Blizzard removed the baseline Seal Twisting that Classic and Classic Anniversary had, and brought it back only through `Twist of Light` at 31 points in this tree. A level-20 or level-30 build labelled for twisting is a build for the weapon and the seal choice, not for the twist.
## Rotation
1. **Always have a seal up**: [[Seal of Righteousness]], or [[Seal of Command]] once talented
2. On anything that will survive several Judgements, **judge [[Seal of the Crusader]] first** for the Holy damage amplifier, then switch back. Blizzard states [[Judgement]] no longer consumes your active Seal, so this costs you mana and nothing else, and the debuff lasts 40 seconds. Blizzard also moved the Judgement half of `Improved Seal of the Crusader` into the class, so the amplifier needs no talent points
3. **[[Holy Strike]]** on cooldown, as mana allows
4. **[[Judgement]]** on cooldown, as mana allows
5. With [[Seal of Command]], **[[Hammer of Justice]] first**: Judgement of Command hits far harder into a stunned target
6. **[[Exorcism]]** against Undead and Demons only
7. Maintain a Blessing and an Aura; top up with [[Holy Light]]
**Multi-target: none until 20.** Then [[Consecration]], and it is expensive: **135 Mana** at rank 1, on an 8-second cooldown. Blizzard limits what it can do on purpose: moderate damage to the first 4 enemies that walk into it, very low damage to everything after that, so Paladin threat stays level with a Warrior's Thunderclap. The thin damage on the fifth target is the design, and Blizzard says the limit keeps Forever out of expansion-era AoE pulling. The client agrees. Its rank 1 does 16 Holy damage over 8 seconds to anything in the area, with an extra 32 over 8 seconds for the first four. A separate beta fix repaired the spell failing to apply that bonus to some targets, along with its inconsistent Z axis.
Later, `Sacred Arbiter` gives [[Holy Strike]] **+20% damage** and makes it refresh every Judgement effect on the target, **even those from other Paladins**, so staying in melee maintains them without re-judging. Blizzard puts it at the new 16-point gold medal, around level 25; the client's thinner text stops at the Judgements on the target without naming whose they are.
## Stat priority
Weapon DPS, then Hit and Expertise, critical strike, **Spell Power**, Strength, Intellect, Agility, Stamina.
Hit and Expertise barely appear on gear at this level, so they are close to irrelevant below 30. A slower weapon makes [[Holy Strike]] hit harder, but raw weapon DPS usually wins.
**Intellect matters much more from 26 points in the tree, at level 35**, where `Champion of the Light` starts converting it into spell damage. Blizzard places the talent in row 6 of this tree.
**It converts up to 30% of your Intellect**, which is Blizzard's talent line and the figure to build against. The designers' note in the same article describes a cut to 60% partway through the beta, and the client at 1.60.1.70374 still reads 100%, so the magnitude has not finished moving — but the direction is agreed on every reading: Intellect becomes a damage stat for a Retribution Paladin who goes that deep. Wowhead, Icy Veins and ClassicWoW.gg may print any of the three figures.
Blizzard confines the effect to damage. A tooltip line that claimed the talent raised Healing is gone, and it never did raise Healing, so read it as spell damage only.
Published gear lists split into a **Spell Power** build and an Attack Power build, and call both competitive at level 20.

## What Forever changed for Retribution
- **Blizzard's Paladin deep dive landed on October 15** and is the first source for everything below. Paladin was the last class to get one, so lines that still name a guide site or the client are lines the dive did not reach.
- **Six new talents:** `Holy Conduit` in row 2, `Sanctified Judgement` in row 3, `Sacred Arbiter` at the new 16-point gold medal, `Champion of the Light` and `Instrument of Law` in row 6, and `Twist of Light` at 31 points.
- **Blizzard's removal list for this tree is two talents**, `Improved Retribution Aura` and `Sanctity Aura`. `Improved Seal of the Crusader` is not on it. Blizzard calls that one altered instead, with its Judgement bonus moved into the class, so the talent survives and only its attack power half still wants points. `Improved Blessing of Might` appears nowhere in Blizzard's lists and the guide sites have it gone, but the client still carries it at 4% per rank over five ranks.
- **`Improved Holy Strike` is still a talent.** Blizzard's dive never mentions it, and the client carries it taking 2 seconds off [[Holy Strike]]'s cooldown. Do not assume it was folded into the class.
- **Seal Twisting is no longer baseline**, and `Twist of Light` at 31 points is the only route back to it. Blizzard built it to be gentler on timing than the Classic original. Seal spells cost 20% less, and replacing a damage-dealing Seal leaves an Echo that makes **your next melee attack apply the replaced Seal's benefit as well as the active one's** — Blizzard's description, and the reason the mechanic is worth 31 points. The client names the four Seals that leave an Echo, [[Seal of Command]], [[Seal of Righteousness]], [[Seal of Fury]] and `Seal of Justice`, and its thinner text covers only the replaced Seal.
- **[[Judgement]] no longer consumes your Seal**, a baseline change Blizzard states for every Paladin. [[Lasting Judgement]] is baseline too, and the client has it adding 10 seconds per rank to `Judgement of Light` and `Judgement of Wisdom`.
- `Vindication` is redesigned. Your damaging melee attacks have a chance to cut the target's Attack Power and to raise your own by up to 3% for 30 seconds. Blizzard tunes the reduction by level to sit close to Demoralizing Shout, and the self-buff is what keeps the talent worth a point against casters. The client's rank 1 is thinner than that: a flat 6 Attack Power off the target for 30 seconds, with no self-buff in the text yet.
- `Sanctified Judgement` gives [[Judgement]] up to a 100% chance to refund up to 60% of the judged Seal's mana cost, and the client reads the full 100% and 60%. It also gates `Vengeance`.
- `Vengeance` sits in row 5 now and requires `Sanctified Judgement`. It stacks three times at 3% damage each, 9% in total, and holds for 30 seconds. Blizzard's earlier version handed out up to 15% for 8 seconds and fell off too often to build around. A beta fix also made it trigger off non-periodic critical strikes only. The client carries no text for the Paladin's copy of this talent, so Blizzard's description is the whole of it.
- `Eye for an Eye` moved down a row and answers every critical strike now, not only spells. **Blizzard puts the reflected share at up to 10% of the damage taken**, against Classic's 30%. The client still reads 15%, and caps the reflect at half the Paladin's health.
- `Pursuit of Justice` is worth up to 15% movement speed for the same points. The client confirms it covers mounted speed and that it does not stack with other speed effects.
- `Instrument of Law` makes `Hammer of Wrath` instant by taking up to a second off its cast, and drops all your threat by 20% while [[Righteous Fury]] is inactive. Blizzard has reduced [[Righteous Fury]]'s own threat bonus without publishing a figure; the client still reads 60%.
- `Repentance` is the 21-point gold medal, so it lands around level 30 rather than 40. The client has it incapacitating a Humanoid for up to 6 seconds, broken by any damage.
- `Crusade` is probably intact. The scope block above explains why that is not certain.
- `Two-Handed Weapon Specialization` reads **2% per rank** in the client's Paladin row, so three points is 6% *(ours, not a source's — the client publishes no rank count)*. Icy Veins and ClassicWoW.gg still carry 3% per rank in their talent data, and Blizzard's dive does not mention the talent.
- [[Consecration]] is baseline at 20 for every Paladin, and Blizzard redesigned it around four targets. See Rotation.
## At level 60
Unknown, and Blizzard frames it as a choice rather than a puzzle. Retribution can twist seals with `Twist of Light` or hybridise into [[Holy Shock]] for Holy damage, and Blizzard positioned [[Holy Shock]] as the alternative so that opting out of twisting is a real build. Neither side has been tested at 51 points. The author of one published guide says *"it's looking like twisting might not be the way to go for Paladin raid DPS specs atm."* Blizzard's closing line is that Retribution stays a strong damage dealer with steadier damage and more group utility, naming `Sacred Arbiter` and its upkeep of other Paladins' Judgements as the example.
